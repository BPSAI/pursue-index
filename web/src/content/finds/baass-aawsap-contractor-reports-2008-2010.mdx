## What the documents are

- **DOW-UAP-D168** (`b87355e7ab051114`): *BAASS Project Management Plan*, December 2, 2008, 49 pages.
- **DOW-UAP-D166** (`9bd42e0157fe4697`): *MUFON Reports Prepared for BAASS*, November 2008.
- **DOW-UAP-D167** (`149f0e075ae4517d`): *Project Capella*, December 2008, a report from Documatica Research to BAASS.
- **DOW-UAP-D169** (`85fd315af4b25993`): *BAASS March 2009 Monthly Report*, dated April 10, 2009.
- **DOW-UAP-D170** (`12a7f37083cf2b87`): *BAASS April 2009 Monthly Report*.
- **DOW-UAP-D171** (`6a7a525ada83a4d6`): *BAASS June 2009 Monthly Report*, dated July 9, 2009.
- **DOW-UAP-D173** (`ffea128d68063d60`): *BAASS November 2009 Monthly Report*.
- **DOW-UAP-D175** (`82f2e60220d94740`): *Summary Report on BAASS UAP Analysis Capabilities*, November 23, 2010.

Release 6 published the government side of the same contract. That included the Statement of Objectives (DOW-UAP-D110), the DIA order HHM402-08-C-0072 (D111) and 37 research papers (D117–D153). The order states that the "Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies, Inc (BAASS) proposal dated 9/3/2008 is incorporated by reference." <Cite card="419ad0ffefd1343c" page={3} q="Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies, Inc (BAASS) proposal dated 9/3/2008 is incorporated by reference." /> Its line items include "Monthly Status Reports" and a "Project Management Plan for Tasks 3.a.1 - 3.a.12" <Cite card="419ad0ffefd1343c" page={3} q="Project Management Plan for Tasks 3.a.1 - 3.a.12" />. The plan and the monthly reports in Release 7 are documents of those two kinds.

All eight are written by the contractor or its subcontractors. People are labeled "AAP Person" with a number, and many passages are redacted. This entry uses no names and does not try to identify anyone behind a label.

## The plan, December 2008

The plan opens with BAASS's view of the stakes: "some of these advanced aerospace craft may represent a threat to the security of the United States including its airspace and nuclear facilities" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={4} q="some of these advanced aerospace craft may represent a threat to the security of the United States including its airspace and nuclear facilities" />. It lists nine projects. Project Physics was "mandated by the DIA" and would produce about 24 position papers through Earthtech <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={8} q="Project Physics was mandated by the DIA to study the current research on all twelve technical areas." />. The others were Engagement, Cooperation, Campus, Database, Oral History, Blue Book Materials, Northern Tier (nuclear-base incursions in the 1960s and 1970s) and Colares (Brazil, 1977).

BAASS stated its method plainly:

> "we deliberately adopted the stance that the most productive way to learn the maximum about the twelve technical areas was to study craft that are already displaying novel behavior in the twelve technical areas." <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={15} q="we deliberately adopted the stance that the most productive way to learn the maximum about the twelve technical areas was to study craft that are already displaying novel behavior in the twelve technical areas." />

It was cooler on the theory papers. "Many of the approaches recommended in the 12 Earthtech Technical Studies are highly theoretical" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={16} q="Many of the approaches recommended in the 12 Earthtech Technical Studies are highly theoretical." />.

Most of the plan is a request list. Project Cooperation "requires the full cooperation between the DIA and BAASS" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={8} q="Project Cooperation requires the full cooperation between the DIA and BAASS and lays out the expected frameworks and specifics of this cooperation." />. The Key Recommendations ask DIA to get clearances for BAASS staff and to obtain gun-camera, ground and satellite film. They ask for Brazilian Air Force files on Colares, medical records included, and for NORAD data on uncorrelated targets. They also ask DIA to secure "the data files known to be excluded from Project Bluebook, on advanced aerospace technology that presently resides at LANL" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={11} q="the acquisition of the data files known to be excluded from Project Bluebook, on advanced aerospace technology that presently resides at LANL" />. Recommendation 9 is framed as a premise: "Under the premise that biological material of potentially non-human origin has been recovered" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={13} q="Under the premise that biological material of potentially non-human origin has been recovered" />, DIA would lobby for access to medical samples. The plan also says "BAASS affiliated personnel have a list of about ten individuals" said to have worked on hardware or biological retrieval programs <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={12} q="BAASS affiliated personnel have a list of about ten individuals who have worked in various parts or have been affiliated with the programs involving retrieval of advanced aerospace hardware or of associated biological material." />.

These are a contractor's assertions in a proposal-style document. The plan gives no evidence for them, and the later reports do not show any of the files, film or samples being delivered. The plan ends with exhibits, among them a 1947 Air Materiel Command memo on "Flying Discs" <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={43} q="AMC Opinion Concerning" /> and a 2002 sealed affidavit. This entry does not read those exhibits.

## The supporting papers

The plan acknowledges deliverables "submitted in fulfillment of contracts 081006-1 to the Mutual UFO Network" (MUFON) <Cite card="b87355e7ab051114" page={5} q="contracts 081006-1 to the Mutual UFO Network" />, among others. D166 is the MUFON set. It is not case data. It is a set of short speculative research papers from a "MUFON Advanced Technology Establishment" on lift, power and other topics. The lift paper says it "concluded that the use of the magnetic field of the earth for propelling vehicles using superconductors has merit" <Cite card="9bd42e0157fe4697" page={2} q="have concluded that the use of the magnetic field of the earth for propelling vehicles using superconductors has merit." />. The power paper proposes buying a facility "to develop the principles of cold fusion" <Cite card="9bd42e0157fe4697" page={7} q="the purchase and subsequent use of an already-designed research facility to develop the principles of cold fusion" />.

D167, by AAP Person 5 of Documatica Research, is the design for Project Capella. It proposes "a data warehousing system that supersedes the individual databases" <Cite card="149f0e075ae4517d" page={6} q="Instead we propose a data warehousing system that supersedes the individual databases, using a standard structure and coding system over the set of files." />, and says this "can be accomplished using a standard Microsoft package such as EXCEL" <Cite card="149f0e075ae4517d" page={6} q="We believe that this can be accomplished using a standard Microsoft package such as EXCEL" />. The rough budget is $610,000 for the first year. The paper criticizes earlier private catalogues for an embedded bias, "usually under the (unstated) assumption that all cases involve spacecraft from extraterrestrial origin" <Cite card="149f0e075ae4517d" page={7} q="usually under the (unstated) assumption that all cases involve spacecraft from extraterrestrial origin, to the exclusion of any other hypothesis." />. Its own six-layer model includes a layer labeled psychic.

## The pipeline: MUFON in numbers

The March 2009 report gives the staffing picture. BAASS received 526 résumés that month and "has thirty three full time employees and seven part time employees" <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={4} q="BAASS has thirty three full time employees and seven part time employees" />. By June 30 the figure was "forty five full time employees and six part time employees" <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={4} q="BAASS has forty five full time employees and six part time employees" />. The March report also describes an approach to the FAA to replace NIDS with BAASS in three FAA publications as the place to report sightings <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={23} q="The three FAA publications that will contain the wording that nominates BAASS as the reporting agency are" />.

MUFON was the field arm. The same report says MUFON "currently has approximately 1000 volunteers" <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={19} q="MUFON has been in existence for four decades and currently has approximately 1000 volunteers who can investigate reports of UAP sightings in all 50 states." /> and that in February 2009 BAASS signed a purchase order for fast notice of significant reports <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={19} q="In February 2009, BAASS executed a Purchase order with MUFON that entitles BAASS to: (a) receive notification of significant reports on UAP sightings from anywhere in the United States within hours" />.

The numbers that came back were small. MUFON's April summary says "Of the 319 United States CMS cases in April, nine of them were category two cases." <Cite card="12a7f37083cf2b87" page={17} q="Of the 319 United States CMS cases in April, nine of them were category two cases." /> There were no category three cases. BAASS wrote that it had "concerns about the current level of productivity from MUFON" <Cite card="12a7f37083cf2b87" page={12} q="BAASS currently has concerns about the current level of productivity from MUFON, especially what appears to be a significant decrease in cases during the month of April." />. In June it set spending against allocation:

> "We note that for the month of February/March, MUFON spent $11,014.37 out of $74,666.67 allocated; in April, MUFON spent $23,803.95 out of $56,000 allocated; and in May, MUFON spent $23,812.00 out of $51,000 allocated." <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={15} q="We note that for the month of February/March, MUFON spent $11,014.37 out of $74,666.67 allocated; in April, MUFON spent $23,803.95 out of $56,000 allocated; and in May, MUFON spent $23,812.00 out of $51,000 allocated." />

It proposed $25,500 a month for July to October <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={16} q="we propose a Phase II funding level between July 1 and October 31, 2009 of $25,500 per month." />. In November it cut again, citing MUFON's "tendency to warehouse significant sums of money" <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={4} q="Hence, for the remainder of the contract, BAASS will be reducing the monthly stipend to MUFON by $10,000." />.

Some of what the pipeline delivered was ordinary. One Modesto, California case from October 2009 was reclassified after a MUFON chief investigator called a nearby hobby shop. He recommended moving the case "from 'Unknown' to 'IFO: Man Made Object' (RC model gliders with blue lights attached.)" <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={10} q="from 'Unknown' to 'IFO: Man Made Object' (RC model gliders with blue lights attached.)" />. This entry does not quote the witness accounts in these reports.

## Capella: a warehouse of existing catalogues

The March 2009 report gives Capella's first count: "234,364 unusual aerial phenomena reports of sightings occurring from 593 B.C. to present time" <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={10} q="The data warehouse currently includes 234,364 unusual aerial phenomena reports of sightings occurring from 593 B.C. to present time." />. Of those, 203,805 came from a single existing catalogue, UFOCAT. BAASS added its own caveat: "BAASS does not believe that the breakdown of reports in the data warehouse accurately reflects the percentage of actual sightings." <Cite card="85fd315af4b25993" page={10} q="BAASS does not believe that the breakdown of reports in the data warehouse accurately reflects the percentage of actual sightings." /> By November 2010 the warehouse "consists of eleven separate databases" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={20} q="The Data Warehouse currently consists of eleven separate databases and their associated supporting documentation in electronic format" />. Most of its volume came from other people's earlier collections.

## What was on hold by November 2009

The plan had asked DIA to unlock archives. The June 2009 report still called Northern Tier "one of the sponsor's top priorities" <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={121} q="Project Northern Tier is one of the sponsor's top priorities" />. In April the project had narrowed to 1975–1976, partly because of "the likelihood of being able to contact witnesses for re-interview" <Cite card="12a7f37083cf2b87" page={34} q="the likelihood of being able to contact witnesses for re-interview" />.

The November 2009 conclusion reports where this stood:

> "Many of the important BAASS projects, including Northern Tier, entry into classified archives, Project Oral History, as well as contacting key people in the military and private sector are on hold because BAASS currently does not have the Need to Know for this information." <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={155} q="Many of the important BAASS projects, including Northern Tier, entry into classified archives, Project Oral History, as well as contacting key people in the military and private sector are on hold because BAASS currently does not have the Need to Know for this information." />

Contacts with other government organizations were "at much too a preliminary stage to assess the likelihood of their success or failure" <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={155} q="These contacts are at much too a preliminary stage to assess the likelihood of their success or failure." />. Field work had moved elsewhere: "A large part of the current investigations at BAASS are focused on the Utah ranch and surrounding areas." <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={155} q="A large part of the current investigations at BAASS are focused on the Utah ranch and surrounding areas." />

Other activity in 2009 was preparation. In June BAASS reported designing an underground vault, in line with "a BAASS priority to pursue and acquire recovered hardware and biological samples from the corporate world and/or from private individuals and groups" <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={7} q="a BAASS priority to pursue and acquire recovered hardware and biological samples from the corporate world and/or from private individuals and groups" />. The report describes a design, not anything stored in it. The same report covers a team sent to Brazil "with specific instructions to maintain a very low profile" <Cite card="6a7a525ada83a4d6" page={101} q="BAASS deployed in a very low key way with specific instructions to maintain a very low profile" />. It came back with copies of files held by private Brazilian groups.

## The 2010 self-assessment

D175 is the closing summary. It says: "Between 2008 and 2010 BAASS was contracted by the Defense Warning Office of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={11} q="Between 2008 and 2010 BAASS was contracted by the Defense Warning Office of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) to create, staff, and equip a new organization" />. It names three resources BAASS built: field sensors and staff, a database, and computational methods. Much of the report is an inventory, from two Canon 5D MK II cameras to a twelve-core server running ANSYS Multiphysics. It is also measured about the reports themselves:

> "BAASS understands a considerable number, possibly a majority, of UAP reports can be explained by simple misidentification of current advanced technology platforms." <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={22} q="BAASS understands a considerable number, possibly a majority, of UAP reports can be explained by simple misidentification of current advanced technology platforms." />

The showcase case is the November 2004 Nimitz encounter. BAASS introduces it as "excerpted from the report of an investigation conducted by personnel assigned to DIA" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={26} q="The following summary is excerpted from the report of an investigation conducted by personnel assigned to DIA of an observation by elements of the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group of UAP off the coast of Mexico in November 2004." />. That DIA report is not itself in this release. The excerpt, as BAASS quotes it, includes the assessment that "The AAV exhibited advanced low observable characteristics at multiple radar bands rendering US radar based engagement capabilities ineffective." <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={32} q="The AAV exhibited advanced low observable characteristics at multiple radar bands rendering US radar based engagement capabilities ineffective." /> The same excerpt also records that a submarine in the group had "no unidentified sonar contacts in the vicinity of the aerial sightings" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={32} q="there were no unidentified sonar contacts in the vicinity of the aerial sightings" />.

BAASS then states its own limits: "Since there was little hard data, most analyses of the incident had to be made using either parametric studies or assumptions based on eye witness testimony." <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={33} q="Since there was little hard data, most analyses of the incident had to be made using either parametric studies or assumptions based on eye witness testimony." /> No duration was known, so "a parametric range from 0.5 seconds to 10 seconds was studied" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={33} q="a parametric range from 0.5 seconds to 10 seconds was studied" /> for the reported drop from about 60,000 feet. The headline figure is one row of that table:

> "An examination of Table 2 at a Δt of 5.0 sec reveals the average velocity of the craft was 12,000 ft/sec (8,182 mph or Mach 10.7)." <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={34} q="An examination of Table 2 at a Δt of 5.0 sec reveals the average velocity of the craft was 12,000 ft/sec (8,182 mph or Mach 10.7)." />

That number is an assumed distance divided by an assumed time. The fluid-flow plots that follow are, in BAASS's words, "provided as a basic demonstration of the CFX fluid flow module" <Cite card="82f2e60220d94740" page={47} q="The cases used are provided as a basic demonstration of the CFX fluid flow module and illustrate some of its capabilities" />. They show what the software can do. They are not a measurement of the object.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming that anything the plan describes exists. That includes files at Los Alamos, recovered hardware, biological material and a list of insiders. These are statements by a contractor seeking access, and nothing in these eight documents shows them confirmed or delivered. By November 2009 BAASS itself reported the archive and interview projects on hold. We are not claiming the Mach 10.7 figure is a measurement. BAASS calls its analysis parametric and built on assumptions, and so do we. The Nimitz assessments are quoted only as BAASS quotes them from a DIA report we have not seen. What the documents do show is narrower: what one contractor told DIA it was doing and what it asked for, what it spent on MUFON, and what it said it had built. Every quotation above is verbatim from the released OCR, pinned to its card and page. The selection and framing are ours.
