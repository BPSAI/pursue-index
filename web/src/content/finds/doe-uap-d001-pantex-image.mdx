## What the card actually contains

Card `aaafecbc47103f4b` is a two-page PDF: pages 5 and 6 of a six-page Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report. The earlier pages of the report — the narrative — are not in the release. What is preserved are the image-plate pages and their captions.

Pantex is the U.S. nuclear-weapons assembly and disassembly facility in Carson County, Texas, managed by Consolidated Nuclear Security, LLC. A UAP incident report from this site is in a different category from a general DOE sighting log: the site's security perimeter is one of the most monitored on the continent.

## What the page surface names that the manifest description does not

**UCNI handling.** Both released pages carry **UCNI** markings at the top and bottom of the page — Unclassified Controlled Nuclear Information. <Cite card="aaafecbc47103f4b" page={1} q="UCNI" /> UCNI is a DOE-specific handling category for unclassified information whose unauthorized dissemination could reasonably be expected to have an adverse effect on the health and safety of the public or common defense and security. It is not "classified" in the national-security sense — it is its own category, governed by the Atomic Energy Act. That this report was marked UCNI rather than left unmarked tells you something about how DOE itself classified the sensitivity of a UAP incident on the Pantex perimeter.

**The (b)(3) exemption with UCNI cited as the underlying statute.** The first released page carries a redaction stamp citing **"(b)(3) (UCNI)"** <Cite card="aaafecbc47103f4b" page={1} q="(b)(3) (UCNI)" /> — FOIA exemption (b)(3) covers information specifically protected from disclosure by another statute. By citing UCNI as the (b)(3) hook, DOE is invoking the Atomic Energy Act as the statutory basis for the redaction, not the more familiar (b)(1) national-security exemption. This is the same legal mechanism the Department uses to control information about nuclear-weapons design.

**Sandia National Labs performed the image enhancement.** The page-6 caption identifies the enhanced imagery as the product of Sandia, not Pantex itself: **"Sandia National Labs Enhanced Images of the Object."** <Cite card="aaafecbc47103f4b" page={2} q="Sandia National Labs Enhanced Images" /> The page-5 caption identifies the original source as a ground surveillance radar tower at the site: **"Image from Ground Surveillance Radar Tower."** <Cite card="aaafecbc47103f4b" page={1} q="Ground Surveillance Radar Tower" /> Two different DOE entities are therefore in the document chain: Pantex's ground-surveillance system is the sensor, and Sandia is the post-processor.

## What the card does not contain

The release does not include:

- The date of the incident (the manifest entry's `incident_date` is null).
- The narrative pages of the report (pages 1 through 4).
- The pre-enhancement radar imagery (only the Sandia-enhanced product is on the released pages, and the images themselves are not OCR-recoverable as text).
- The classification authority for the underlying UCNI determination.

A reader who clicks through sees two image-plate pages with UCNI banners and the Consolidated Nuclear Security, LLC management stamp. The actual content of the incident — what was seen, when, by what sensor configuration, with what disposition — is in pages that were not released.

## Why both fragments are in this archive

The two released pages are thin in body text but the *handling surface* of a primary source is itself evidence. The presence of UCNI markings, the choice of (b)(3) UCNI as the redaction citation, and the naming of Sandia as the image-enhancement laboratory are three facts about how DOE processed this incident that survive on the released pages even though the narrative does not. A future release of the full six-page report would slot directly into this card's page sequence at pages 1–4.

DOE-UAP-D001 is one of three DOE documents in the 5/22/26 release (alongside D002 and D003). It is the only one of the three whose surface ties a UAP incident to the Pantex perimeter and to Sandia's analytical chain.
