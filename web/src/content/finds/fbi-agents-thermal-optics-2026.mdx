## What the documents are

- **FBI-UAP-D032** (`0aa52db3ec97c002`): a two-page FD-302 interview report with a special agent, "Slow-moving Objects," 2026.
- **FBI-UAP-D033** (`0e40c556fc072f29`): a two-page FD-302 that transmits a special agent's signed written statement "verbatim", "Thermally Elevated Aerial Object," 2026.
- **FBI-UAP-PR007** (`f634a8f291eb0280`): a video card paired in the catalog with D032. It has no text layer, so nothing here quotes it.

The records are redacted throughout: names, locations, dates, the agency component and equipment make are all blacked out, and the second statement refers to the same observation area as the first statement. We do not try to identify either person, the site or the operation. What survives is the sequence of what each observer did and said he saw.

Both first pages end with the standard line that the document "contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of the FBI" <Cite card="0aa52db3ec97c002" page={1} q="This document contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of the FBI." />.

## The first record: an RF hit, then a thermal optic

The agent in D032 says up front that "He has no aviation expertise" <Cite card="0aa52db3ec97c002" page={1} q="no aviation expertise." />. That caveat belongs on everything that follows. At about 1604 on the day, he was alerted to a radio-frequency detection at 10,000 feet MSL lasting 5 to 10 seconds, with an unknown bearing, and then asked to look toward 58 degrees <Cite card="0aa52db3ec97c002" page={1} q="At approximately time 1604 he was alerted to an RF detection at" />.

Looking that way through a thermal optic he saw a black object that was not moving fast, then a second one. The report says the objects were moving together at the same speed, and gives their polarity in the thermal display in a single sentence:

> "Both were black hot." <Cite card="0aa52db3ec97c002" page={1} q="Both were black hot." />

The observation lasted about five to ten minutes. The agent could not estimate altitude or speed <Cite card="0aa52db3ec97c002" page={1} q="is unable to estimate an altitude or speed." /> and the report says it was difficult to determine direction of travel given the distance. He was the only person at his location to see the objects and the only one who used the optic. He took photographs and video, and another agent looked at the photographs. Whether that clip is the one in PR007 is something the catalog's pairing suggests, not something the text says.

## The second record: a helicopter for scale

D033 reproduces a statement written by a different agent about the next evening, at about 2100 hours. He says he

> "observed a thermally elevated aerial object in the same general western" sector, along a similar heading to the object of the night before. <Cite card="0e40c556fc072f29" page={1} q="observed a thermally elevated aerial object in the same general western" />

It appeared roughly two ridge lines further west, which the agent says limited what he could make out. He also filmed a U.S. Army Black Hawk helicopter operating nearby, and explains why: to establish a visual and thermal reference point "for scale, flight characteristics, and comparative size assessment" <Cite card="0e40c556fc072f29" page={1} q="point for scale, flight characteristics, and comparative size assessment." />. His comparison: the

> "unidentified aerial object appeared to be approximately half the size of the" Black Hawk. <Cite card="0e40c556fc072f29" page={1} q="unidentified aerial object appeared to be approximately half the size of the" />

and he adds, immediately, "This size comparison is an estimate only" <Cite card="0e40c556fc072f29" page={1} q="This size comparison is an estimate only" />. He closes by saying he could not determine "whether it was a fixed-wing, rotary-wing, or other unmanned aerial system" because of distance and limited observable detail <Cite card="0e40c556fc072f29" page={2} q="whether it was a fixed-wing, rotary-wing, or other unmanned aerial system" />. The object continued southbound and was lost behind terrain and distance.

## What these records are, and are not

Our reading is that the interest here is method. Both observers worked from a cue, used a thermal optic, wrote down a bearing and a time, took a reference recording, gave a size as an estimate, and declined to name what they saw. Those habits are why the statements are readable. This is our observation about how the records are written, not a judgment of what was in the sky.

It is also worth noting what the records do not contain: no range from any sensor, no altitude for the thermal objects, no identification of the RF source, and no statement of what the RF detection was supposed to have detected. The two nights are linked by the second agent's own words, not by any measurement.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming the objects were unmanned aircraft, drones, or anything else, and we are not claiming they were not. The first agent had no aviation expertise, by his own record, and could not estimate altitude or speed. The second agent states his size comparison is an estimate. A thermal image at long range cannot reliably fix size without a known distance, and neither record gives one. We also do not claim the two nights describe the same object: the second statement says the object it saw was farther west and along a similar heading, which is a resemblance and not an identification.
