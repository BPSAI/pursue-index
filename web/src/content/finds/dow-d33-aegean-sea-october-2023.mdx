## What the document is

Card `3746998b8c506e5c` is **DOW-UAP-D33, Mission Report, Greece, October 2023** — a 7-page MISREP filed by the 33 SOS, declassified by MG Richard A. Harrison on 22 January 2026 and approved for release to AARO under USCENTCOM MDR 26-0019. The mission was an ISR sortie out of LGLR on the Greek mainland: takeoff at 2339Z on 26 October 2023, landing at OJMS at 1309Z on 27 October — 13 hours 30 minutes of mission time, 6 hours 29 minutes of FMV. The UAP observation lasted three minutes. <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={1} q="MISSION HOURS" />

The same `card_id` appears twice in the manifest — one row for the PDF, one for the paired AARO **Unresolved UAP Report PR-34** — because the corpus content-addresses by source URL and both rows point at the same asset. After the [D32 Syria entry](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024) — three clips in a single document — D33 is the cleaner shape: one observation, one clip, one short GENTEXT paragraph. The interest here is not in form-anatomy abstraction (D32 walks that) but in how *terse* the aircrew were and how visible their own epistemological care is in the fields they did fill in.

## The form fields, as filled in

The MISREP's UAP section sits on pages 6 and 7. Most of the kinetic and signature fields are left blank or marked `UNK`. The fields the aircrew did commit to are short and specific:

> "**UAP Maneuverability Observations:** Sharp 90 degree turns" <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={6} q="Sharp 90 degree turns" />
>
> "**UAP Physical State:** Solid"
>
> "UAP Propulsion Means: UNK"
>
> "UAP Under Intelligent Control (yes/no; if yes, describe): NO"
>
> "**Observer Assessment of UAP: Benign**"

"Sharp 90 degree turns" is a striking phrase, and a reader should hold it carefully — it is the **aircrew's own characterization** entered into a free-text field, not an instrument measurement. The corresponding kinematics block on page 7 leaves altitude, depth, and trajectory blank (each marked `Estimated` accuracy with no value), and fills only one number:

> "**Kinetic Velocity Accuracy:** Estimated
>
> **Kinetic Velocity:** 80 MPH" <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={7} q="80 MPH" />

What the document is willing to commit to numerically is one estimated velocity figure.

Where D33 differs from D32 inside the same form is also worth noting. The Physical State dropdown — `Solid | Gas | Plasma | UNK` — reads "Plasma" on the Syria card and "Solid" on this one. Same constrained menu, different selections by different aircrews looking at different things.

## The aircrew's own description

The most quotable line on the document is also the most editorially careful one. In the GENTEXT/UAP block on page 7, where the form opens up into prose, the aircrew were given a "UAP Description" field — size, shape, color, markings, recognizable features — and chose to write:

> "**UAP Description (e.g., size, shape, color, markings, recognizable features):** SEEMINGLY CIRCULAR, TOO SMALL TO MAKE OUT DETAILS" <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={7} q="SEEMINGLY CIRCULAR" />

That is the editorial gem of the card. The witness was offered the form's most descriptive slot and used it to tell the reader where their own confidence ran out. *Seemingly* circular, not circular. *Too small to make out details*, not "smooth, featureless, metallic." The hedge is on the page; the aircrew calibrated their own visual confidence inside the field meant to extract it.

The narrative GENTEXT immediately following is similarly disciplined:

> "AT 0035Z [callsign] WAS EN ROUTE TO THEIR TARGET WHEN THEY SPOTTED A UAP FLYING JUST ABOVE THE SURFACE OF THE OCEAN WATER. THE UAP TOOK MULTIPLE 90 DEGREE TURNS AT AN ESTIMATED 80 MPH. AT 0038Z, [callsign] LOST THE UAP FROM THEIR FEED." <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={7} q="MULTIPLE 90" />

Three minutes — 0035Z to 0038Z. *Multiple* turns, the count is not given. *An estimated 80 MPH*, the qualifier is in the prose. *Lost the UAP from their feed*, not "lost the target" or "broke contact" — the loss is described as a sensor event, not a kinematic one. Every quantitative claim is hedged on the page where it appears.

## What AARO wrote about the video

The paired **PR-34 Unresolved UAP Report** describes the 2 minute 57 second FMV clip from the clip side, with AARO's standard prose discipline:

> "00:04: An area of contrast enters the sensor field-of-view from the bottom left quarter of the screen.
>
> 00:07-00:19: The area of contrast moves back and forth horizontally across the field-of-view as the sensor pans to track it.
>
> ...
>
> 02:22: The area of contrast becomes indistinguishable against the background, and the reticle drops its lock.
>
> 02:27-02:57: After losing lock, the sensor rapidly cycles zoom levels and contrast thresholds."

AARO's vocabulary stays at "area of contrast" throughout — no "object," no "craft," no "vehicle." The 90-degree turns the MISREP narrates do not surface in this text as turns; they surface as horizontal motion across the field-of-view between 00:07 and 00:19. The closing boilerplate disclaimer — "should not be interpreted as reflecting an analytical judgment, investigative conclusion, or factual determination" — is the same one AARO appends to every PR description.

## A second mission report, two days later

A separate MISREP, **DOW-UAP-D35** ([card `c788c17e8cc32230`](/card/c788c17e8cc32230)), covers a 29 October 2023 mission also over the Aegean Sea. Its description records the UAP as "small and circular" and as flying near the surface of the ocean; its paired video, PR-35, is 24 seconds long and shows the area of contrast becoming indistinguishable as the background scene transitions from water to land. D35 is a separate card and a separate observation. The MISREPs do not assert that the two events involved the same phenomenon, and this entry does not either. Two filings, same general area, two days apart. That is what the corpus contains.

## What the document does not say

Five things the D33 MISREP and the PR-34 description do **not** establish, that a careful reader should hold separately from the things they do:

1. **"Sharp 90 degree turns" is not an instrument measurement.** It is a phrase the aircrew typed into a free-text field on a structured intake form.
2. **"80 MPH" is explicitly an estimate.** The form's `Kinetic Velocity Accuracy` slot reads `Estimated` for that figure. <Cite card="3746998b8c506e5c" page={7} q="Kinetic Velocity Accuracy" />
3. **The document does not identify the object's origin or propulsion.** `UAP Propulsion Means: UNK`. `UAP Under Intelligent Control: NO`.
4. **The document does not characterize the object as exotic.** *Observer Assessment of UAP: Benign.*
5. **The document does not claim D33 and any other mission's UAP are the same object.** The D35 cross-reference above is to a separately-filed report covering a different date, not a continuation of this one.

## Why this card is in this archive

The [D32 Syria entry](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024) is about how a structured intake form constrains what an aircrew can say. D33 is the cleanest version of the same form: one observation, one short GENTEXT paragraph, one paired clip, most kinetic and signature fields left empty.

The methodological lesson on the page is not "Sharp 90 degree turns" — that phrase, by itself, is the easiest thing to lift out of context and over-quote. The lesson is what the aircrew did with the "UAP Description" field. Given a slot for size, shape, color, markings, and recognizable features, they wrote `SEEMINGLY CIRCULAR, TOO SMALL TO MAKE OUT DETAILS` — and stopped. The hedge is in the form. A reader who treats that line as the shape of an honest aircrew description has the right baseline for reading every other UAP narrative this corpus contains.
