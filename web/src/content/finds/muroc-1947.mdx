## What the documents say

On Tuesday, July 8, 1947, two unusual sightings happened a few hours apart over Muroc Army Air Field — the high desert flight-test installation that Hollywood and the Army Air Forces both like, now known as Edwards AFB.

The same day, *2,000 miles east in New Mexico*, the public information officer at Roswell Army Air Field issued — and within hours retracted — a press release announcing the recovery of a "flying disc."[^roswell-context]

The Muroc events stayed quieter, but five Army Air Force personnel filed sworn affidavits about them within two weeks. The FBI investigation file, opened on July 8 by Air Defense Command and forwarded to FBI San Francisco on August 26, 1947, is in this corpus. <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={60} />

## The 09:30 sighting

1st Lieutenant **Joseph C. McHenry**, Billeting Officer at Muroc, gave his statement on July 11, 1947. It opens with a line that has the specific texture of contemporaneous testimony rather than later reconstruction:

> "Someone will have to show me one of these Disc before I will believe it." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={66} q="show me one of these Disc" />

He had said this minutes earlier in the Post Exchange. He then walked back to his office:

> "Looking up, as I always do I observed the aircraft, and looked slightly to the left, whereupon I observed two (2) silver objects of either a spherical or disc-like shape, moving about three hundred (300) miles an hour, or perhaps less, at approximately eight thousand (8000) [feet]…" <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={66} q="silver objects" />

McHenry called other personnel out from the Billeting Office. Three of them — **T/Sgt Joseph Ruvolo**, **Secretary Jannette Marie Scott**, and **S/Sgt Gerald E. Nauman** — gave their own sworn statements on July 14, 1947, all describing the same two silver disc-like objects, all giving the same speed range (300–400 mph) and same altitude (~8,000 ft), all noting the absence of any audible engine sound.

Scott's affidavit:

> "On Tuesday morning 8 July 1947, at approximately 10:00, while typing some cards, and taking care of my routine work, Lieutenant McHenry called me, along with T/Sgt. Joseph Ruvolo and S/Sgt. Gerald E. Nauman, to the front of B.O.Q. 'A' Bldg. Pointing up in a direction directly above me he asked us to explain what we saw. There were two, silver colored disc like objects flying toward Mojave, California, one directly back of the other, at a speed of about 300 to 400 miles per hour, having an altitude of approximately 8000 feet." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={71} q="silver colored disc" />

Nauman's:

> "I have 20-20 vision and I am positive these two (2) objects could not have been Aircraft, weather balloons or birds." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={72} q="20-20 vision" />

McHenry's account adds a second observation moments later:

> "Upon further investigation, two (2) of us at the same time sighted another object of a silver spherical or disc-like nature at approximately eight thousand (8000) feet, traveling in circles over the North-end." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={67} q="traveling in circles" />

He notes specifically that the object's "circle was too tight and too severe a plane to be any aircraft that I know of." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={67} q="too tight and too severe" />

## The noon sighting

Around 12:00, **Major Richard R. Shoop**, Office of the Chief of Tech. Engineering Division, was called outside by **Colonel Gilkey**, the Commanding Officer of Muroc. Shoop's statement, taken July 11, 1947:

> "I observed between five (5) and eight (8) miles to the North what appeared to be a thin metallic object. It appeared to be metallic because the method in which it was flying caused the sun to reflect like an apparently aluminum colored surface. The object moved from an intermediate altitude in an oscillating fashion, almost to the surface of the ground and then started climbing again. It climbed to a fairly high altitude and moved off slowly into the distance. The object appeared to be the size of a pursuit airplane but did not have the shape of a conventional plane. The time that the object was in view was approximately eight (8) minutes. This same object was seen by my wife at the same time." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={64} q="thin metallic object" />

Colonel Gilkey himself, interviewed orally by Captain Harry D. Black (Intelligence Officer, Muroc) on August 11, 1947, later believed his observation might have been "paper" — a more conservative read that's preserved in the file alongside the original sworn affidavits. <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={63} q="paper" />

## What the documents don't say

These are eyewitness reports, not photographs, not radar tracks. Nobody at Muroc on July 8, 1947 had a camera pointing at the sky for the right eight minutes. The cover memo from Lt. Col. Donald Springer (4th Air Force, Hamilton Field, August 18, 1947) marks the case status as "Pending" and notes "No further investigation of these incidents is being considered by this headquarters." <Cite card="99c12a9c49a91750" page={61} q="No further investigation" />

The objects were not identified. The witnesses were not contradicted. The case was closed.

## Why it's in the corpus

The Muroc incident sits inside FBI file **62-HQ-83894 Section 3** — one of ten sections of the FBI's omnibus UFO file, ~1,900 pages spanning 1947 to 1968. The investigation memo is dated 1947; the FBI declassification stamp is dated *May 24, 2007*. Sixty years inside the filing system before declassification; another nineteen before the Department of War surfaced it as part of PURSUE in 2026.

If a UFO researcher is going to challenge how solid the public record actually is, the Muroc affidavits are exactly the kind of primary source that document review needs to engage with. Multiple witnesses. Sworn statements. Same day. Same place. Same description. No identification.

[^roswell-context]: This is well-documented context, not a claim from the corpus. The Roswell press release ("RAAF Captures Flying Saucer On Ranch in Roswell Region") was published in the *Roswell Daily Record*, July 8, 1947; the retraction came hours later from 8th Air Force HQ in Fort Worth.
