## What the documents are

- **EOP-UAP-D001** (`ea1e7c4a981bdd8b`): one page. A Foreign Broadcast Information Service (FBIS) transcript of a Radio Tupi broadcast from Rio de Janeiro, 2330 on 8 November 1963, with handwritten notes at the top and bottom.
- **DOS-UAP-D001** (`09ab9032c16fbaa2`): one page. A State Department incoming telegram of 14 November 1963 from the embassy in Rio de Janeiro, addressed to the consulate in Salvador, with copies passed to Defense and NASA.
- **DOS-UAP-D002** (`942f77cebc0a64c6`): two pages. State Department Airgram A-628, 20 November 1963, from the embassy in Rio, signed by the Scientific Attaché.

The Executive Office document is a copy that reached the National Aeronautics and Space Council; its handwritten annotations are partly illegible and carry initials. We do not interpret them beyond what they say. Officials named in the telegram and airgram are named in their official roles only.

## The broadcast

The radio text is short. It reports, from "the municipality of Conde, in Baia", that

> "A LARGE METAL SPHERE FELL IN THE CENTER OF THE CITY, GOUGING A HOLE" four meters deep. <Cite card="ea1e7c4a981bdd8b" page={1} q="LARGE METAL SPHERE FELL IN THE CENTER OF THE CITY, GOUGING A HOLE" />

The sphere has window-shaped openings, "COVERED WITH THICK GLASS", and inside, the report says, is "A HUMAN BODY DRESSED IN HEAVY" clothing bearing inscriptions that cannot be deciphered <Cite card="ea1e7c4a981bdd8b" page={1} q="COVERED WITH THICK GLASS. INSIDE IS A HUMAN BODY DRESSED IN HEAVY" />. The population is said to be intrigued and the authorities informed. This is a radio report relayed through a monitoring service. It carries no named source and no verification.

At the top, a handwritten note reads "a Curious report." <Cite card="ea1e7c4a981bdd8b" page={1} q="a Curious report." /> A second note at the bottom asks State to let the space council know if the embassy follows up with any information.

## The embassy asks

On 14 November the embassy's scientific attaché sent a telegram to the consulate in Salvador. The radio link the night before had been weak, so he asked the consulate to confirm his understanding of an "unqualified negative" on the report that a large metal manned balloon had been found near Salvador, with a dead crewman aboard <Cite card="09ab9032c16fbaa2" page={1} q="CONFIRM OR CORRECT MY UNDERSTANDING OF YOUR UNQUALIFIED" />. He added what he had also heard: that an object had been sighted aloft around that date and tentatively identified as a weather-type balloon, but that

> "REPEAT NO SUCH OBJECT HAD BEEN FOUND AGROUND." <Cite card="09ab9032c16fbaa2" page={1} q="REPEAT NO SUCH OBJECT HAD BEEN FOUND AGROUND." />

The telegram asks that the message be passed to Defense, for the Air Force, and to NASA.

## The closing airgram

The airgram of 20 November reports the consul's reply of 14 November. The consul wrote that the story of a strange object descending near Conde was

> "apparently fabricated in Rio no such objects seen by" anyone in the area, according to newspaper reporters and others who rushed there <Cite card="942f77cebc0a64c6" page={1} q="apparently fabricated in Rio no such objects seen by" />

The Secretary of Public Security, the consul added, speculated that the object might have been a weather balloon, but no such sighting was verified. The consul also sent two newspaper clippings. One reports that the Ministry of Aeronautics categorically denied any strange object in Conde. The other is titled "The Airship that wasn't there" <Cite card="942f77cebc0a64c6" page={1} q="Airship that wasn't there" />.
The airgram ends with the newspaper reporter's own aside. The only newsworthy part of the episode, he said, was that he had sent a telegram to his paper from the federal telegraph office in Conde on "an old morse code transmitter" and had a reply from Salvador five minutes later <Cite card="942f77cebc0a64c6" page={1} q="worthy aspect of the episode is that it produced an astounding" />.

The airgram's subject line gives the date of the sighting as 8 November "1964" [sic]. The cover and all other dates in the document are 1963. We read that as a typing error in the original.

## What the three documents show together

A radio report becomes a transcript, the transcript gets a handwritten note and a request for follow-up, the embassy asks the consulate to confirm, the consulate checks with reporters and officials, and the embassy writes it up and routes it to Defense and NASA. The airgram is filed under the State Department's science subject code for Brazil, the same channel and subject family as [the 1963 memo from the space council to State on extraterrestrial-contact policy](/finds/nasc-state-extraterrestrial-policy-memo-1963), which was written four months earlier. We link the two because they sit in the same channel, not because either mentions the other. For a broader look at how ordinary explanations are tested, see [What's Not UAP](/finds/whats-not-uap).

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming anything fell in Conde in November 1963. The consul and the press report that nothing did, and the radio story has no named source. We are also not drawing conclusions from the initials on the radio transcript. The documents show how the U.S. government handled a rumor; they do not show that the rumor had a basis.
