## What the documents are

Card `be533a4ab886eb11` is **"USPER Statement about UAP Sighting"** — a three-page FBI document released as part of Release 01. The narrative form (chronological prose, third-person summary of a single witness's first-hand account, indented "Comments" block at the close) is consistent with an FBI **FD-302** interview summary, which is how the FBI's institutional grammar records what an agent was told. The classification banner runs **SECRET//NOFORN** on all three pages (the page-1 OCR captures the banner as `SECRET//NOFORM`, an artifact of the banner underline; pages 2 and 3 OCR cleanly).

Card `ccd932b4f40e8de7` — **"FBI Photo B7"** — is a single grayscale frame: a helicopter in the upper-center-right of the display, a central crosshair reticle, tick-mark scales running `5`, `10`, `15` horizontally on both sides of center. The OCR yields essentially no text — only the tick numerals and a corrupted system timestamp `12/31/99 18:10:02` (the source-system clock was not set). The image-description note recorded for that card describes the frame as a "Grayscale video frame of a helicopter in the upper center-right, overlaid with a central crosshair and tick-mark scale labeled 5, 10, and 15 on both horizontal sides." <Cite card="ccd932b4f40e8de7" page={1} q="Grayscale video frame of a helicopter" /> The manifest description carried with the image notes "a second, smaller, dark circular object… visible below the reticle" and that "an accompanying mission report was not provided."

Both cards carry `redacted: true` in the manifest. The 302 reads as essentially nothing *but* redactions in its proper nouns — and the structure of what the redactor chose to leave in vs. take out is itself the most useful reading of the document.

## What the redactor left in, and what they took out

The 302 anonymizes by category. Each redacted entity is replaced with a bracketed role token, and the categories are kept distinct from each other across all three pages:

- `[WITNESS 1 (a senior US intelligence official)]` and `[WITNESS 2 (a senior US intelligence official)]` — two people, same role descriptor, agencies redacted.
- `[FEDERAL PARTNER 1]` through `[FEDERAL PARTNER 4]` — four separate federal partner agencies, none named.
- `[STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION]` — the operator of the helicopter at the center of the chase, distinct from the federal partners and from the military aircraft.
- `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]` — a class of aircraft (five of them, on an unrelated training mission) that appears later in the timeline.
- `[OPERATIONS CENTER]`, `[FACILITY]`, `[MOUNTAIN RANGE NAME]`, `[SITE CODE NAME]`, `[LOCAL TOWN]`, `[NEARBY TOWN NAME]`, `[ROAD NAME]` — every place name redacted.
- `[CALL SIGN 1]`, `[CALL SIGN 2]` — both helicopter call signs redacted.
- `[COORDINATES]` — every coordinate string, on every page.
- The incident date itself: the document opens with `On [DATE], at approximately 1700 hours…`. <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={1} q="senior US intelligence official" />

What the redactor *did* leave in: Zulu-style timestamps to the minute (1700, 1751, 2050, 2052, 2141, 2149, 2202, 2207, 2218, 2227, 2228, 2233, 2241, 2249, 2252, 2257, 2306, 2316, 2320), the count of personnel and aircraft, the sensor types in use at each observation (FLIR, NVG, naked eye), and the verbatim radio-relayed language LP/OP used to describe the orb on FLIR (`"super-hot"`, in quotation marks in the document itself).

The document is therefore readable as a precise timeline of *who saw what on which sensor* — with every proper noun, every place, and the date stripped out.

## The witness, as the document characterizes them

The narrative is told from the perspective of WITNESS 1. Both WITNESS 1 and WITNESS 2 are introduced with the same role descriptor:

> "On [DATE], at approximately 1700 hours [WITNESS 1 (a senior US intelligence official)], [FEDERAL PARTNER 1] and [FEDERAL PARTNER 2], accompanied by [WITNESS 2 (a senior US intelligence official)], and two pilots from [STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION], departed the [OPERATIONS CENTER]…" <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={1} q="senior US intelligence official" />

"Senior US intelligence official" is the exact phrase, and it is the only descriptor either witness is given. The agency, and therefore the witness's military-versus-civilian status, is among the things the redactor took out. The intelligence community spans civilian career services and uniformed components; this document does not say which side either witness sits on, and a reader who wants to stay inside what the document supports should hold "senior US intelligence official" at exactly the level of specificity the redactor allowed.

## The sensor chain, in the order the document records it

The structural feature of this 302 is that it carefully segregates which platform observed which event on which sensor. A reader following the chronology can trace the chain:

**1700 hours — composite departure.** Two senior intelligence officials, two FEDERAL PARTNER agencies, and two pilots from a STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION lift off from the OPERATIONS CENTER in a STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION helicopter, `[CALL SIGN 1]`, for a daytime aerial search. The mission is prompted by prior eyewitness reports of orbs/lights and personnel "thuds as if something has fallen and hit the ground." <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={1} q="thuds as if something has fallen" />

**2141 hours — handover to LP/OP.** `[CALL SIGN 1]` lifts off from refueling and heads toward debris spotted by a **Listening Post/Observation Post** — `[FEDERAL PARTNER 4]` personnel "using Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) and Night Vision Goggles (NVG)". <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={1} q="Forward Looking Infrared" /> The LP/OP is a ground observation post operated by a different federal partner than the agencies aboard the helicopter; its instruments — not the helicopter's — are what carry the next phase of the encounter.

**2202 hours — the "super-hot" relay.** `[CALL SIGN 1]` arrives at LP/OP-provided coordinates and "spotted a possible aircraft on the horizon moving away to the west." Then:

> "LP/OP confirmed the sighting then relayed they spotted an orb under FLIR and described as 'super-hot' hovering at ground level before heading [BEARING] from LP/OP." <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={1} q="super-hot" />

Two structural details to preserve. The word `"super-hot"` is in quotation marks in the document — it is the relayed FLIR-observation language from LP/OP, transcribed by the 302 as quoted speech, not the 302's own characterization. And the observation is a **thermal description of what LP/OP saw on the FLIR display**, not an instrument temperature reading; the document does not say what temperature `"super-hot"` corresponds to.

**2207 hours — the military aircraft appear, separately.** Page 2 opens by introducing a different platform category:

> "JOC reported five [MILITARY AIRCRAFT] from [REDACTED] were in the air space conducting a training mission." <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={2} q="MILITARY AIRCRAFT" />

The 302 is consistent across all three pages about this distinction: `[CALL SIGN 1]` is a STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION helicopter; the `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]` are a separate flight of five aircraft on an unrelated training mission, who later "JOC relayed they contacted… enroute to assist, eta two minutes." The helicopter and the military aircraft are two different things, with two different operators, in two different roles in the timeline.

**The 20-mile leg, as LP/OP reports it.** This is the most-quoted detail and the one the document is most precise about. After `[CALL SIGN 1]` is on station and finds nothing on its own sensors:

> "Once on station, [CALL SIGN 1] searched the location with FLIR, NVG, and the naked eye but did not locate the orb. LP/OP reported the orb gained elevation, came within ten feet of [CALL SIGN 1] and then headed east to [REDACTED]. [CALL SIGN 1] moved to intercept but LP/OP reported the orb continued southeast and estimated it to be past [ROAD NAME], some 20 miles from the current position at [REDACTED]. [CALL SIGN 1], unable to match speed, broke off pursuit…" <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={2} q="20 miles from the current" />

The paragraph rewards a slow read. The helicopter, with FLIR, NVG, and naked-eye observers, "did not locate the orb" on its own sensors. The orb's gaining elevation and ten-foot proximity to `[CALL SIGN 1]` are recorded as **LP/OP-reported** events. The orb's southeast continuation and 20-mile estimate are **LP/OP's estimate of distance from `[CALL SIGN 1]`'s position**, made over the period after the helicopter had lost contact, not a continuous track flown by the helicopter. The helicopter "unable to match speed, broke off pursuit" — but the pursuit it broke off was one it had not, on its own instruments, been able to reacquire.

The co-pilot does record a brief second-sensor observation in that same window — "the co-pilot reported seeing under NVG something emerge from the two objects and then travel in a different direction and a high rate of speed" — but this is an NVG note, not a tracked extended observation.

## The eight further structured observations

The 20-mile leg is one item on a much longer timeline. Across the rest of pages 2 and 3, between 2218 and 2257 hours, the 302 records eight further structured observations of orbs, each by `[CALL SIGN 1]` with WITNESS 1 on the naked eye and the pilots on NVG. The recurring pattern is the document's own vocabulary:

> "The orbs flared up one at a time in a horizontal formation and after approximately ten – fifteen seconds, flared down in the opposite order and lost visual." <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={2} q="flared up one at a time" />

Variations on that sentence — sometimes with `ten seconds` instead of `ten – fifteen`, sometimes with `three distinct orbs in a triangle formation`, sometimes with `four or five in total below the original two` — recur five times across the two pages, each anchored to a Zulu timestamp and a position relative to a redacted site code name. The form of the observation is part of what makes it sit oddly with the structured-form MISREPs in the same corpus: the 302 has no checkbox for `flared up`, no numeric kinetic block, no Observer Assessment field. It is prose.

WITNESS 1's closing comment on page 3, set off from the timeline as a separate "Comments" section:

> "Pilots indicated they were recording however many sightings were above the helicopter which was outside of the helicopters FLIR camera angle. Some things I could not see with the naked eye, but I recall the pilots calling out sightings. The orbs appeared to break off from [CALL SIGN 1] and pursue the [MILITARY AIRCRAFT]." <Cite card="be533a4ab886eb11" page={3} q="break off" />

The closing sentence is WITNESS 1's interpretation of what was observed; the 302 records the interpretation as the witness's words, without adjudicating it.

## The FD-302 grammar vs. the MISREP grammar

The 302 and the DOW MISREPs ([D23](/finds/dow-d23-arabian-gulf-october-2023), [D32](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024), [D33](/finds/dow-d33-aegean-sea-october-2023)) all sit in the same Release 01 corpus, and all are modern (2020s) primary-source UAP documents. They commit to different things because they are different document types.

A MISREP is **a structured form filled out by aircrew**. Its commitments are check-box selections and numeric block entries. When a MISREP says `UAP Physical State: Solid`, it is the aircrew's selection from the menu `Solid | Gas | Plasma | UNK`. When it says `Kinetic Velocity: 320 MPH`, it is an estimated number written into a labeled field. The form's range — what fields it offers, what menus it constrains the selection to — is part of what makes any given entry readable.

An FD-302 is **a prose narrative summary, written by an agent, of what a witness said**. It does not have field menus; the structure is chronological. Its commitments are at a different layer — *what the document chose to write down as direct quotation*, *what redaction category each anonymized noun was sorted into*, and *what observational language the agent transcribed as the witness's own*. There is no `UAP Physical State: Solid` field here; there is `"super-hot"` in quotation marks, attributed to LP/OP, on FLIR. There is no `Observer Assessment: Benign` field; there is the closing-comments paragraph in which WITNESS 1 says the orbs "appeared to break off from [CALL SIGN 1] and pursue the [MILITARY AIRCRAFT]" and the document records that as the witness's interpretation, not as a finding.

The two grammars also redact differently. A MISREP that records an Iranian guard call leaves the call sign of the friendly aircraft as `[callsign]` and otherwise commits to numbers (FL230, 158 KIAS) and proper-noun adversary identifications (`IRANIAN AIR DEFENSE`). A 302 takes out every proper noun in the witness's account and leaves the *categories* (`STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION`, `FEDERAL PARTNER 4`, `MILITARY AIRCRAFT`) so the structure of the operation is legible even when none of its participants are. A reader interested in modern UAP documents has both grammars to work with, and they are usefully different ways of writing down what happened.

## The companion image — what it does and does not show

Card `ccd932b4f40e8de7` is a single grayscale frame depicting a helicopter under a central crosshair reticle with tick scales — visually consistent with an LP/OP FLIR display imaging an in-air helicopter, though the document chain does not assert this. The image carries no embedded metadata except the corrupted system timestamp; OCR returns essentially nothing.

The 302 and the photo are both FBI-released, both dated `Late 2025`, both carry `redacted: true`, and both depict a helicopter-adjacent UAP scenario. They are **not** structurally paired in the manifest's `pdf_pairing` field — they are not declared by the corpus to be the same incident, the way DOW MISREPs are paired to their AARO videos. Editorially they appear to be a 302 and its companion frame; structurally the corpus records them as two independent FBI Release 01 cards. The helicopter visible in the frame **could** be `[CALL SIGN 1]`, viewed through LP/OP's FLIR, with the manifest-described "second, smaller, dark circular object" being the orb described in the 302's narrative. The document chain does not say so, and this entry does not assert it.

## What the document deliberately withholds

A short index of what the 302 takes out, organized by category:

- **Who the witnesses are.** Two senior US intelligence officials. The agency or service of either is redacted.
- **Which federal agencies are involved.** Four separate federal partners are referenced as `[FEDERAL PARTNER 1]` through `[FEDERAL PARTNER 4]`. None is named.
- **Who flew the helicopter.** A `[STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION]`. The 302 does not say which.
- **What kind of military aircraft assisted.** Five `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]` on a training mission. The aircraft type and operator are redacted.
- **Where this happened.** The operations center, the facility, the mountain range, the site code names, the nearby towns, and the road are all redacted. Every coordinate is `[COORDINATES]`.
- **When this happened.** The opening line is `On [DATE]`. The Zulu-style timestamps and the manifest `incident_date: Late 2025` are what survives the redaction.
- **What bearing and what destination the orb headed toward.** Every cardinal-direction follow-on (`heading [BEARING]`, `headed east to [REDACTED]`) is bracketed.

What the document does **not** withhold: the sensor types in use, the timestamps, the personnel counts and categories, the verbatim radio-quotation of LP/OP's `"super-hot"`, and the structure of the observation chain — which is enough to read the document as what it is.
