## What these documents are

Where the DOW *Mission Reports* — [D23](/finds/dow-d23-arabian-gulf-october-2023),
[D32](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024), [D33](/finds/dow-d33-aegean-sea-october-2023) —
are the *output* of the modern UAP-collection pipeline (a structured MISREP, filed after the
fact by a squadron intelligence shop), the three cards here are the *intake*: **Range Fouler
Debrief Forms**, the one-page questionnaire an aircrew fills out in their own words while the
encounter is still fresh.

- **DOW-UAP-D090** (`02fa613f21662149`) — Range Fouler Reporting Form, Eastern US, 2019, classified **SECRET//REL TO USA, FVEY**.
- **DOW-UAP-D089** (`042b3e178a68a19c`) — Range Fouler Debrief Form, Eastern US, 2020, **SECRET/NOFORN**, carrying the clean AARO/NDAA declassification stamp.
- **DOW-UAP-D091** (`24f4178ed9082f2c`) — Range Fouler Debrief Form, Atlantic, 2020, **SECRET//NOFORN**.

The form itself is worth reading before the encounters are, because it tells you the program is
built for *systematic* collection, not one-off curiosity. It instructs the aircrew that identity
will be discarded — "Absolutely no identifying information for aircrew or squadron will be recorded"
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={1} q="Absolutely no identifying information for aircrew or squadron will be recorded" /> —
and it is emphatic about the physical evidence: "Please ensure all display tapes are ripped for
the entire time of interaction"
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={1} q="Please ensure all display tapes are ripped for the entire time of interaction" />,
saved and uploaded to a repository. This is an evidence-preservation instrument with a de-identification
step baked in — the same institutional posture the [DOW numbered-incident corpus](/finds/dow-incidents-1-100)
shows the 1947–48 Army Air Forces reaching for with its printed CHECK-LIST, now with sensor tapes attached.

## The strong one: D090, out-flown after 28 years

The 2019 Eastern-US report is the reason to read this release. The observer was not in a fast
jet — the platform was "onboard a civilian King Air"
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="onboard a civilian King Air" /> — with two other crewmembers
and two pilots aboard, on a mission between sortie sets. What he describes is not a distant light
but an object whose behavior he sets explicitly against his own experience:

> "...I noticed an object with flight characteristics unlike anything I had seen in my 28 years of performing..." <Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="unlike anything I had seen in my 28 years of performing" />

He tracked it for roughly ten to fifteen seconds — a small object below the aircraft, traveling in
a straight line, opposite their heading, at high speed — before the crew "turned on the recorder to
provide the attached" video.
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="turned on the recorder to provide the attached" />
Then the kinematics: when he zoomed in for resolution, "the object's speed took out of my FOV"
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="the object's speed took out of my FOV" />
— it accelerated out of his field of view and he could not reacquire it, even after zooming back out.
Only on post-flight analysis did a shape resolve: the object "appeared to be rectangular."
<Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="be rectangular" />

Two things keep this from being a single-observer anecdote. There is an attached video, referenced
in the narrative. And there is corroboration from qualified witnesses:

> "Others with equal or more experience were also unsure as to what this object might be." <Cite card="02fa613f21662149" page={2} q="Others with equal or more experience were also unsure as to what this object might be." />

That is the shape of a genuine unknown: multiple experienced observers, a recording, and a candid
statement that nobody on board could identify it.

## The multi-sensor one: D089

The 2020 Eastern-US pilot report (rank O-4, filed from the pilot's seat) is terser, but it adds the
one thing a single eyewitness account can't supply on its own — a second sensor. The narrative notes
the "apparent object also captured with" a second system
<Cite card="042b3e178a68a19c" page={1} q="also captured with" /> (the corroborating sensor is redacted).
The object appeared small, moving at constant speed and direction; the shape was indiscernible except
for one detail the pilot was sure of — "the bottom was reflective."
<Cite card="042b3e178a68a19c" page={1} q="the bottom was reflective" />
On the form's checkbox grid, only **Metallic** and **Reflective** are marked
<Cite card="042b3e178a68a19c" page={1} q="Metallic" /> — the aircrew committed to a material impression
and declined to commit to a shape.

D089 is also the card that carries the release's provenance in the clear. Its second page is the
declassification stamp: Department of the Navy, DUSN(M) DRMD, "Authority: FY 24 NDAA, now codified at
44 U.S.C. 2107"
<Cite card="042b3e178a68a19c" page={2} q="now codified at 44 U.S.C. 2107" />, released in part under
AARO's FY2024 NDAA §1843 authorities and "E.O. 13526, section 1.4(a)"
<Cite card="042b3e178a68a19c" page={2} q="E.O. 13526, section 1.4(a)" /> and 1.4(e). This is the same
AARO declassification lane that produced the MISREP finds — the intake forms and the mission reports
are coming out of the same statutory pipe.

## The honest one: D091, which the crew thought was a balloon

The third card is included precisely because it probably is not a mystery, and the record is better for
saying so. The 2020 Atlantic report (rank O-3, filed by a WSO, at dusk) describes a single contact the
crew closed on as it drifted with the wind, generally southbound, with no maneuvering: "We noted no
maneuvers or change in direction from the object."
<Cite card="24f4178ed9082f2c" page={2} q="We noted no maneuvers or change in direction from the object" />
The object was "a darker, maroonish color"
<Cite card="24f4178ed9082f2c" page={2} q="a darker, maroonish color" />, "approximately 12-15ft in height."
<Cite card="24f4178ed9082f2c" page={2} q="approximately 12-15ft in height" />

And the crew reached for the obvious explanation themselves — while being careful not to assert it:

> "Structurally, it appeared as a large, somewhat deformed balloon, but we were unable to verify that as we passed at the merge." <Cite card="24f4178ed9082f2c" page={2} q="a large, somewhat deformed balloon" />

An object that drifts with the wind, doesn't maneuver, is roughly balloon-sized and balloon-shaped, and
whose observers' first instinct is "balloon," is most likely a balloon. What the form captures well is
the crew's own epistemic caution — they wrote down the prosaic hypothesis and then flagged that they
could not confirm it: "unable to verify that as we passed at the merge."
<Cite card="24f4178ed9082f2c" page={2} q="unable to verify that as we passed at the merge" />
That same care — a witness volunteering the limits of their own observation — is exactly what makes
[D33's](/finds/dow-d33-aegean-sea-october-2023) "too small to make out details" credible, and it is why
D091 belongs in the release: a UAP-reporting system that only surfaced the inexplicable cases would be
less trustworthy, not more.

## The companion video set

These three transcribed forms do not stand alone. The same DOW release includes **19 companion
DOW-UAP-PR "Unresolved UAP Report" video clips**, spanning 2015–2025 — the modern equivalent of the
FMV clips paired with the MISREP finds ([D32's](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024) PR-31/32/33,
[D23's](/finds/dow-d23-arabian-gulf-october-2023) PR-26/27). They are video-only assets with no OCR to
quote, so this entry does not cite them line-by-line, but they are the moving-image half of the same
collection effort the debrief forms feed.

## Where this sits in the record

Read together, the modern-Navy DOW finds now trace the whole apparatus: the intake form the aircrew
fill out (here), the structured Mission Reports the squadrons file
([D23](/finds/dow-d23-arabian-gulf-october-2023), [D32](/finds/dow-d32-syria-october-2024),
[D33](/finds/dow-d33-aegean-sea-october-2023)), the AARO video reports paired to them, and the
data-integrity seams that show up when the government re-publishes its own material
([D20, replaced between tranches](/finds/dow-uap-d20-iraq-2023-replaced)). What the Range Fouler forms
add is the human end of it — an experienced observer using the words "unlike anything I had seen," a
crew that names a second sensor, and a crew that names a balloon. None of these is a claim about what
the objects are. Every quotation above is verbatim from the released OCR and hash-pinned to its card and
page for verification. The strongest of them, D090, is exactly the kind of case AARO exists to run down:
multiple qualified witnesses, an attached recording, and an object that out-performed the people watching it.
