## What the documents are

- **DOW-UAP-D176** (`0e2f21236a2289fa`): *Triangular UAP AARO Memorandum for Record, 2026*, 3 pages. It is on Office of the Under Secretary of Defense (Intelligence and Security) letterhead. The subject line calls it an "All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) Witness Debrief" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={1} q="All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) Witness Debrief" />, and the signature block calls it an "Official Record of Joint Witness Debrief" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="Official Record of Joint Witness Debrief" />.
- **FBI-UAP-D043** (`ee657817d068d59b`): *FD-302, Unidentified Aerial Object Observation Report, 2026*, 11 pages. Pages 1 and 2 are the FD-302 and its continuation sheet. Pages 3 to 11 are image-only and carry no machine-readable text. The text pages do not say what those images are, so this entry does not describe or cite them.

The release manifest pairs the two files: each lists the other in its `pdf_pairing` field. Both carry the incident date `June, 2026` and the location `Western United States`. Neither text gives the day. In both, the date is withheld.

The redactions are marked with the release's exemption codes, 1843(a)(1)(B), 1843(a)(1)(C), 1843(a)(2) and 1843(a)(3). Names, the date, the interviewers and the place are withheld. This entry refers to the witnesses only by the roles the documents give them, and makes no attempt to locate the site.

## What the agents wrote (FD-302)

The FD-302 is not an interview summary in the usual sense. It says the FBI received "a written statement co-signed by" two special agents, and that the statement "is provided verbatim below for ease of reading" <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="The statement will be attached to this FD-302 and is provided verbatim below for ease of reading" />. Everything after that is the agents' own first-person text. (The manifest description calls the file a record of an interview. The page itself says it is a received statement.)

The statement opens with a call from another agent. At "approximately 1923 hours", that agent:

> "called out a detection for a thermally elevated aerial object located west of his position at an estimated bearing of 251 degrees." <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="called out a detection for a thermally elevated aerial object located west of his position at an estimated bearing of 251 degrees." />

The writer and a partner then ran from the eastern side of their observation post to the western side "in an attempt to collect further data related to the detection from Observation Post 6" <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="in an attempt to collect further data related to the detection from Observation Post 6." />. What they saw:

> "almost immediately observed a substantial, nonreflective, dark black triangular object most resembling a delta wing drone." <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="almost immediately observed a substantial, nonreflective, dark black triangular object most resembling a delta wing drone." />

They estimated the wingspan "at approximately nine to fifteen feet", the depth at six to nine feet and the thickness at one to two feet <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="We estimated the object's wingspan at approximately nine to fifteen feet, its depth at six to nine feet, and its thickness at one to two feet." />. The writer looked through a handheld thermal device while the partner tracked the object with the naked eye. Through the thermal device it:

> "appeared hotter than ambient temperature with a completely consistent thermal signature across the entire airframe and no visible exhaust gases" <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="appeared hotter than ambient temperature with a completely consistent thermal signature across the entire airframe and no visible exhaust gases" />

The flight path, in the statement's order: the object rose vertically over a ridge line "approximately 3.2 miles west of our location" <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="approximately 3.2 miles west of our location on a heading of about 268 degrees." />, climbed to about 200 feet above it, then switched to horizontal flight "at a very significant velocity, approaching our position, then descending southeast" <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={2} q="at a very significant velocity, approaching our position, then descending southeast" />. They lost sight of it at about 1.5 miles. The statement closes with two sentences:

> "The total duration of the sighting was approximately three seconds." <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={2} q="The total duration of the sighting was approximately three seconds." />

> "Throughout the observation, neither [REDACTED] nor I detected any auditory output from the unmanned aerial system." <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={2} q="neither [REDACTED] nor I detected any auditory output from the unmanned aerial system." />

The form's footer carries the FD-302's standard line: "This document contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of the FBI." <Cite card="ee657817d068d59b" page={1} q="This document contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of the FBI." /> The FBI pages add no analysis of their own.

## What AARO recorded

AARO's memorandum lists two "Primary Interviewees", SOURCE 1 and SOURCE 2, and an "Additional Corroborating Witness", SOURCE 3. The interviewers' names are withheld. Before the account begins, the memorandum adds a caveat in its own voice:

> "This report reflects the reporter's interpretation at the time of the event." <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={1} q="This report reflects the reporter's interpretation at the time of the event." />

Its overview says that at 2023L "federal law enforcement agents documented an unidentified craft presenting as a matte black triangular shape of extremely low aspect ratio that was nonreflective under the observed lighting conditions" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={1} q="federal law enforcement agents documented an unidentified craft presenting as a matte black triangular shape of extremely low aspect ratio that was nonreflective under the observed lighting conditions" />. It gives a wingspan of thirteen to fifteen feet <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={1} q="It had an estimated thirteen-to-fifteen- foot wingspan" />, a fuselage of six to nine feet and a thickness of one to two feet.

The chronology sets SOURCE 1 and SOURCE 2 at the primary observation post, with their tripod-mounted sensors facing east. The light was late dusk, after sunset. Then:

> "SOURCE 3, positioned at the secondary observation post scanning westward with long-range thermal equipment, detected an elevated object transiting over the ridge line" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={2} q="SOURCE 3, positioned at the secondary observation post scanning westward with long-range thermal equipment, detected an elevated object transiting over the ridge line" />

SOURCE 1 and SOURCE 2 moved across the peak. The tripod thermal system stayed behind. SOURCE 2 carried binoculars and a handheld thermal monocular. The memorandum splits what followed into three timed phases.

**Phase I, vertical ascent, about ten seconds.** A slow vertical climb of two to three hundred feet with no tilt or wobble. The memorandum puts the range "between approximately 1.4 and 3.2 miles west of the primary observation post by terrain cross-reference" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={2} q="Range to the object was bounded between approximately 1.4 and 3.2 miles west of the primary observation post by terrain cross-reference." />.

**Phase II, hover, about twenty seconds.** The overview puts it this way: "the craft instantaneously arrested all vertical motion and entered a stable hover for an estimated twenty seconds" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={1} q="the craft instantaneously arrested all vertical motion and entered a stable hover for an estimated twenty seconds" />. On the thermal monocular:

> "In the infrared spectrum it displayed a spatially uniform thermal signature across the entire airframe without localized thermal concentrations, exhaust gradients, or discrete heat sources associated with propulsion." <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={2} q="In the infrared spectrum it displayed a spatially uniform thermal signature across the entire airframe without localized thermal concentrations, exhaust gradients, or discrete heat sources associated with propulsion." />

**Phase III, departure, about two to three seconds.** A banking turn and a descent into a canyon. The memorandum notes that the speed figure is the witnesses' comparison and nothing more: they estimated it against "a military fighter jet", and "No instrumented speed measurement was obtained." <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="No instrumented speed measurement was obtained." /> The monocular lost the object when it accelerated out of the narrow field of view.

The memorandum records no sound in any phase. It also includes a collector note on the two primary witnesses:

> "SOURCE 1 and SOURCE 2 have extensive operational experience working alongside government agencies and academic studies utilizing unmanned aerial systems (UAS)" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="SOURCE 1 and SOURCE 2 have extensive operational experience working alongside government agencies and academic studies utilizing unmanned aerial systems (UAS)" />

The note says they "regularly observe conventional aircraft operating around the observation site". It also says that on that hilltop "even a conventional Group 1 drone, high-torque quadcopter motor, or rotary-wing aircraft operating at a distance of 1.4 miles would have been clearly audible" <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="even a conventional Group 1 drone, high-torque quadcopter motor, or rotary-wing aircraft operating at a distance of 1.4 miles would have been clearly audible" />.

## Side by side

The two texts agree on a lot: a dark, matte, nonreflective triangle; a vertical rise over a ridge line to about 200 feet; an even heat signature with no exhaust; no sound; a fast departure downward out of sight. They differ on the points below. Every entry in the table is taken from the documents.

| | FD-302 (agents' written statement) | AARO Memorandum for Record |
|---|---|---|
| Time | "at approximately 1923 hours" (p. 1) | "2023L" (pp. 1, 2) |
| Wingspan | "approximately nine to fifteen feet" (p. 1) | thirteen to fifteen feet (p. 1) |
| Height above ridge | "approximately 200 feet" (p. 1) | "approximately 200 to 300 feet" (p. 2) |
| Duration | "approximately three seconds" in total (p. 2) | about 10 s ascent, about 20 s hover, 2 to 3 s departure (pp. 2–3) |
| Heat | "hotter than ambient temperature" and "completely consistent" (p. 1) | "spatially uniform"; no hot spots, exhaust gradients or heat sources (p. 2). It does not say hotter than ambient. |
| What it is called | "delta wing drone"; "the unmanned aerial system" (pp. 1–2) | "unidentified craft" throughout (pp. 1–3) |

Two of these rows carry most of the weight. In the agents' statement, the whole event lasts about three seconds. In AARO's debrief it lasts about half a minute, two-thirds of it a stationary hover that the written statement does not mention. And the agents' own statement uses drone vocabulary, while the debrief, which also records their UAS experience, uses "unidentified craft". Neither document mentions the other, and neither explains the difference. The one-hour gap in the time is not explained either. This entry does not offer an explanation.

## What was done afterward

AARO's memorandum records one follow-up:

> "A ground survey of the canyon drainage conducted the following morning located no wreckage, ground scarring, or vegetation damage." <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="A ground survey of the canyon drainage conducted the following morning located no wreckage, ground scarring, or vegetation damage." />

It also records what was not found. There was no "impact signature, smoke, dust, or physical disturbance". The witnesses reported no physiological effects. "No electromagnetic interference, signal degradation, or anomalous sensor readings were recorded on personal or agency equipment." <Cite card="0e2f21236a2289fa" page={3} q="No electromagnetic interference, signal degradation, or anomalous sensor readings were recorded on personal or agency equipment." /> The FD-302 records only that the statement was received and would be attached. Neither document records a conclusion about what the object was.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming the object was a drone, or that it was not. The agents' own statement calls it one. AARO's record calls it an unidentified craft. AARO also notes that no instrumented speed measurement exists and that its account reflects the reporter's interpretation at the time. We are not claiming either document is the more accurate one. We have set out where they differ and left the differences as the documents leave them.

The release's description of DOW-UAP-D176 says more than its three pages do. It says the observations took place over two days and that the memorandum records a second observation on the following evening. It says AARO considers the incident to warrant further investigation because the reported features and flight behavior closely match those of other incidents in the western United States. It also mentions analytic support material. None of that appears in the released text. The pages describe a single evening's event, a ground survey the next morning, and a collector note, and they do not mention other incidents. Those statements come from the releaser's description, and the pages released do not support them. They may refer to material that was not released. We do not know. The nine image-only pages of the FBI file are not cited here because they carry no text this entry can quote.
