## What the documents are

The main source is the question-and-answer session at the end of the March 1952 briefing by Edward J. Ruppelt. The briefing itself is covered in [Ruppelt's March 1952 Briefing](/finds/ruppelt-briefing-march-1952). The cards:

- **DOW-UAP-D154** (`e7f5382c5349c6e8`): the 19-page edited transcript. The Q&A runs from p13 to the end.
- **DOW-UAP-PR160** (`208d494dece9b545`): the audio recording. This archive's machine transcript of it is five pages.
- **DOW-UAP-D105** (`fec5b139eb7b0a4f`): the *U.S. Air Force Flying Discs File, 1952*, used here for one November 1952 memo about *True* magazine.

**Who is speaking.** The transcript does not name speakers in the Q&A. Two Air Force officers answer: Ruppelt, and a senior officer who calls him "Lieutenant Ruppelt". The answer about *Life* below refers to "Colonel Kirkland" in the third person, which suggests Ruppelt is speaking. We can't confirm that from the text, so this entry attributes the Q&A to "the briefers" throughout.

## Life: "print what we told them to"

An audience member asks whether the Ground Observer Corps has been brought in. The answer covers the coming *Life* article as well:

> "Now, in two weeks, a week from this coming Friday, Life is coming out with an article on these objects, and we've been working closely with Life, and they're supposedly are going to print what we told them to." <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={16} q="we've been working closely with Life, and they're supposedly are going to print what we told them to." />

("they're supposedly are" [sic], as spoken and transcribed.)

The briefer goes on: "I hope they do. They're going to put it on a factual basis" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={16} q="I hope they do. They're going to put it on a factual basis." />. The stated aim is that the article "may tend to take the mysteriousness and hysteria out of the thing" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={17} q="may tend to take the mysteriousness and hysteria out of the thing" />.

Asked whether magazines might hold back facts for effect, the briefers say *Life* in particular "has a lot of information that we do not have on different sightings" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={17} q="Life magazine has a lot of information that we do not have on different sightings." />. The magazine checked its cases with the Air Force, and the famous old cases favored by "the science fiction writers" are ones "Life is going to leave out" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={17} q="Life is going to leave out." />.

## The Ground Observer Corps: don't alert them

The audience question is direct: "How about the ground observer corps? They have 100,000 people, don't they?" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={16} q="How about the ground observer corps? They have 100,000 people, don't they?" />. The answer:

> "we're afraid maybe if we alert that number of people, then everybody in the United States will get excited again. That's the thing we're trying to keep out of this thing is this excitement business." <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={16} q="we're afraid maybe if we alert that number of people, then everybody in the United States will get excited again." />

The audio's machine transcript has the same sentence, with "would get excited again" <Cite card="208d494dece9b545" page={4} q="then everybody in the United States would get excited again." />.

## True: "no end of trouble"

The same answer looks ahead to *True* magazine:

> "It will be slanted the other way. It's going to be written by one of these science fiction writers, and it should give us no end of trouble." <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={17} q="these science fiction writers, and it should give us no end of trouble." />

"We haven't been able to get True to cooperate with us at all" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={17} q="to get True to cooperate with us at all." />, the briefer adds, before softening it: they probably would cooperate, but "they sell a sensational item".

Eight months later the Air Force Flying Discs file shows the relationship in practice. A 28 November 1952 Memorandum for Record from the Directorate of Intelligence sets out the task: "To prepare a non-military letter to Mr. Ken W. Purdy, Editor of TRUE magazine" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={13} q="To prepare a non-military letter to Mr. Ken W. Purdy, Editor" />. Purdy had mailed General Samford, the Director of Intelligence, the December issue with a flying-saucer article by Donald Keyhoe. Samford had already thanked him for an earlier issue, and "A precedence was thereby established" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={13} q="A precedence was thereby established." />. The reply was drafted by staff: "It is written in the first person from General Samford" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={13} q="It is written in the first person from General" />. Purdy's cover letter is on the next page <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={14} q="In view of your kindness in commenting" />.

In our reading, the two cards together show a project that expected trouble from *True*, and a Directorate that answered *True*'s editor with a courteous letter drafted by staff in the general's name. The documents do not say whether the December 1952 article is the one the briefers were bracing for in March.

## Classification: "we carry it as confidential"

The last exchange is about classification. Asked whether the subject is "still classified top secret, since Life is coming out with it", the briefer answers:

> "No, uh, we've, we actually, we carry it as confidential, the overall project." <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={18} q="we actually, we carry it as confidential, the overall project." />

The audio transcript agrees <Cite card="208d494dece9b545" page={4} q="we carry it as confidential, the overall project." />. The reason for wanting a downgrade is the magazine: "to get it dropped down to restricted now because Life is going to come out with a lot of material we've given them that is classified, or was classified at one time" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={18} q="to get it dropped down to restricted now because Life is going to come out with a lot of" />. The briefer describes the failure mode they want to avoid. A paper asks for results, is told they are classified, and runs "a big story about, 'The Air Force just picked up a spaceship somewhere and they won't tell us about it.'" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={18} q="a big story about, 'The Air Force just picked up a spaceship somewhere and they won't" />

The second briefer adds one area that stays classified: "that's where we have reports on people" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={18} q="There is one other area that's classified, and that's where we have reports on people." />, meaning background checks on witnesses' reliability. The session ends on a tone of openness: "we're not trying to hide anything, we're trying to get help" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={19} q="we're not trying to hide anything, we're trying to get help." />. One briefer also concedes that "newspapers surpass the Air Force in their intelligence channels" <Cite card="e7f5382c5349c6e8" page={19} q="newspapers surpass the Air Force in their intelligence channels." />.

## Where this sits in the archive

Ten months after this session, the Robertson Panel recommended a program of public education to strip UFOs of their special status: [The 1953 Robertson Panel](/finds/robertson-panel-1953-strip-special-status). This Q&A is an earlier and more informal version of the same instinct. Here it is not a policy. It is a small project office trying to keep excitement down while it asks the public for reports. The same instinct, applied to one piece of evidence, shows up in February 1953 in [The Tremonton Film (1952–53)](/finds/tremonton-film-1952-1953). A later, blunter version is in [Blue Book, the Committee, and "Off the Hook" (1965–1967)](/finds/blue-book-committee-off-the-hook-1965-1967).

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming that the Air Force dictated *Life*'s article or that anything in it was false. The phrase "print what we told them to" is informal, and the briefer follows it at once with "I hope they do". The documents here do not show what *Life* printed. We are not claiming the project was hiding evidence either. The same session asks the public for reports and says outright that it is "not trying to hide anything". What these pages show is a project that thought about public reaction as part of its job: which audiences to alert, which magazine to work with, and what classification would cause the least suspicion. Speaker attribution in the Q&A is uncertain, and we have not assigned any of it to Ruppelt by name. Every quotation above is verbatim from the released text, pinned to its card and page. The selection and framing are ours.
