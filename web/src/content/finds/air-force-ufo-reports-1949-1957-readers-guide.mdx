## The form: what each field is for

Nine of the fourteen are on AF Form 112, the Air Intelligence Information Report. The header asks for Subject, Area, From, Date of Report, Date of Information, Evaluation, Prepared By and Source. The summary box instructs: "Enter concise summary of report. Give significance in final one-sentence paragraph." <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={1} q="Enter concise summary of report. Give significance in final one-sentence paragraph." /> In the Saudi report the source is the "Crew, B-52 Aircraft Number 394" <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={1} q="Crew, B-52 Aircraft Number 394" />, and its last line reads "The unidentified aircraft appeared on the scope where no aircraft were expected." <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={1} q="Significance: The unidentified aircraft appeared on the scope where no aircraft were expected." />

**Date of Report and Date of Information differ**, often by weeks. Guam is dated 8 February 1957 for information of 15 January <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={1} q="8 Feb 57 15 Jan 57 B-4" />, about a sighting on 31 December. The Italian report is dated 15 December 1953 with information dated only "August" <Cite card="3d74932e77e7868e" page={1} q="15 December 1953 August D-4" />, for a sighting in August 1952.

**The second format is the numbered list set by AFR 200-2**: the object (shape, size, color, number, formation, features, trail, sound), its course, manner of observation, time, location, observers, weather, other activity, evidence, interception, air traffic, and the preparing officer. The Canadian letter is "submitted in accordance with AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953" <Cite card="e5c4575f838f0175" page={1} q="The following report is submitted in accordance with AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953" />. The Indiana letter uses the list "In lieu of AF Form 112b as required by AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953" <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={1} q="In lieu of AF Form 112b as required by AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953" />. Guam and Morocco put the list inside Form 112. For a radar case the blanks fill with "N/A", and "Height finder was out so information concerning angle of elevation was not available." <Cite card="fc95f65e81965edf" page={2} q="Height finder was out so information concerning angle of elevation was not available." />

## The evaluation code is about the source

The Evaluation box holds a letter and a number: A-1 (D160), B-2 (D155, D163), B-4 (D156), C-3 (D161, D162), D-4 (D157), and "NONE" or "None" (D159, D179). These cards never define the scale. How they use it shows that it grades the source and the information, not the object.

The only A-1 is Morocco, whose source is a radar detachment. Its finding is that the return "might have been caused by" anomalous propagation, ship-borne jamming, or "Geese or Storks in flight", and that "Logical deduction is that one of above mentioned possibilities is the cause for this radar return." <Cite card="fc95f65e81965edf" page={1} q="Logical deduction is that one of above mentioned possibilities is the cause for this radar return." /> The top grade went to a report the division explained away.

The D-4 is Italy. The attaché wrote that the witness "appeared to be a very intense individual and, while not exactly fanatic, I believe that this title comes very close to fitting him." <Cite card="3d74932e77e7868e" page={1} q="appeared to be a very intense individual and, while not exactly fanatic, I believe that this title comes very close to fitting him." /> The witness himself wrote: "I dare not affirm - and it would be presumption on my part - that the sphere was occupied by non-human beings" <Cite card="3d74932e77e7868e" page={4} q="I dare not affirm - and it would be presumption on my part - that the sphere was occupied by non-human beings" />.

Sweden is a B-2 from "Press and Personal Contact" <Cite card="81fe3a1b9cd4bf42" page={1} q="Press and Personal Contact" />: a translated newspaper interview with the captain, plus an informant's character sketch of him. The attaché notes that his informant doubts the story, and that "the news- papers report that the Defense Staff is continuing its investigation of the incident." <Cite card="81fe3a1b9cd4bf42" page={3} q="the news- papers report that the Defense Staff is continuing its investigation of the incident." /> In the Berlin log extracts the source is an air traffic controller and the box reads "EVALUATION NONE" <Cite card="2570557981d64cc7" page={1} q="EVALUATION NONE" />.

## Four ways a report ends

### Unexplained, on a condition: Guam, 31 December 1956

An F-86D pilot in the Guam holding pattern saw a blinking white light pass below him. Thinking it was a B-47, he turned toward it, and "The object proceeded to turn with the aircraft." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={2} q="The object proceeded to turn with the aircraft." /> It climbed above him and circled. "The interceptor was placed in afterburner and turned into the object trying to head it off." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={2} q="The interceptor was placed in afterburner and turned into the object trying to head it off." /> He could not close. After about ten minutes the "pilot had to break off and return to Andersen Air Force Base due to low fuel remaining." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={2} q="pilot had to break off and return to Andersen Air Force Base due to low fuel remaining." /> The squadron's summary of his effort: "Tried to get type and shape and to observe the object. Succeeded only in keeping it in sight." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={3} q="Tried to get type and shape and to observe the object. Succeeded only in keeping it in sight." />

The liaison officer who prepared the report checked for balloons. A lighted weather balloon had been launched at 1432Z and was last tracked at 20,000 feet at 1500Z, seventy minutes before the sighting. Questioned again, the pilot "did not believe that the UFOB was a weather balloon because the UFOB maneuvered with the F-86D and accel- erated past his aircraft." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={3} q="he did not believe that the UFOB was a weather balloon because the UFOB maneuvered with the F-86D and accel- erated past his aircraft." /> "If the balloon had malfunctioned and leveled off or descended, it could possibly explain the reported UFOB." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={4} q="If the balloon had malfunctioned and leveled off or descended, it could possibly explain the reported UFOB." /> The summary states the conclusion:

> "Unless the lighted balloon which was launched prior to the sighting mal- functioned and remained at a relatively low altitude, the Unidentified Flying Object can not be readily explained." <Cite card="338995394c698b33" page={1} q="Unless the lighted balloon which was launched prior to the sighting mal- functioned and remained at a relatively low altitude, the Unidentified Flying Object can not be readily explained." />

That sentence does not say the object was a balloon, and it does not say it was unexplained. It names the one condition under which the balloon works and leaves that condition unchecked.

### Radar only: Saudi Arabia and Morocco

A B-52's tail-gun scope held a return for five to seven minutes. "The unidentified aircraft gave a smaller return on the scope than a simultaneously received return produced by a second B-52 aircraft" <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={1} q="The unidentified aircraft gave a smaller return on the scope than a simultaneously received return produced by a second B-52 aircraft" />. It went off the scope when the bomber banked, and "The disappearance may have been caused by the change in direction of the B-52" <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={1} q="The disappearance may have been caused by the change in direction of the B-52" />. Page 2 is the scope sketch <Cite card="cb5426fe2a0b512d" page={2} q="A-3A GUNNERY SCOPE PRESENTATION" />.

In Morocco a TPS-1D showed V, W and M shapes whose "Number-Varied with sweep from one (1) to twelve (12)." <Cite card="fc95f65e81965edf" page={2} q="Number-Varied with sweep from one (1) to twelve (12)." /> The explanation came from division headquarters, not the site: "Although radar maintenance personnel at the Site of observation have offered no possible cause for the sighting" <Cite card="fc95f65e81965edf" page={3} q="Although radar maintenance personnel at the Site of observation have offered no possible cause for the sighting" />, division electronics personnel listed the three causes.

### Set aside

On Little Diomede a local resident saw a light on the southern tip of Big Diomede, USSR. "The sighting is unusual because persons have not been seen previously in that location" <Cite card="f52425adaa32c4f2" page={1} q="The sighting is unusual because persons have not been seen previously in that location" />. The preparing officer raised signaling toward Alaska, then withdrew it: "There is no evidence to support a conclusion that this was the purpose of the light." <Cite card="f52425adaa32c4f2" page={3} q="There is no evidence to support a conclusion that this was the purpose of the light." /> At Gambell the bearing ruled out land, and the summary allows that "a possibility exists that it could have come from an unidentified ship operating in or near U. S. Territorial Waters." <Cite card="d3cb04b8e4d55dc1" page={1} q="a possibility exists that it could have come from an unidentified ship operating in or near U. S. Territorial Waters." />

The Berlin report is a set of air traffic control log extracts. A British airliner saw "what appeared to be red colored rockets, being fired approximately one (1) mile in front of him." <Cite card="2570557981d64cc7" page={1} q="what appeared to be red colored rockets, being fired approximately one (1) mile in front of him." /> For another pilot's stationary red light, "the Soviets checked on this reported red light and could not account for it." <Cite card="2570557981d64cc7" page={1} q="the Soviets checked on this reported red light and could not account for it." /> A Pan American pilot said that three nights earlier "he observed ground to air firing." <Cite card="2570557981d64cc7" page={1} q="he observed ground to air firing." /> The office "has been unable to contact Pan American Airlines" <Cite card="2570557981d64cc7" page={2} q="this office has been unable to contact Pan American Airlines" /> pilot for more. No conclusion is drawn.

Canada closes on "Cause unknown." <Cite card="e5c4575f838f0175" page={4} q="Intelligence Officer, Captain. Cause unknown." /> The Indiana letter was prepared by a sergeant because there was "No officer assigned at the time of report contact." <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={4} q="No officer assigned at the time of report contact." /> Air traffic was "Impossible to verify without exact date" <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={4} q="Impossible to verify without exact date" />.

## Titles and dates: where the release's labels and the pages disagree

- **D157 (Italy).** The title says 1952 and the file name says 1953, the report year. The cover sheet gives "the 28th of August 1952, in Sassari, Sardinia." <Cite card="3d74932e77e7868e" page={1} q="the 28th of August 1952, in Sassari, Sardinia." /> The witness's own newspaper piece says "Yesterday evening, 27th of August, at 2145 hours" <Cite card="3d74932e77e7868e" page={2} q="Yesterday evening, 27th of August, at 2145 hours" />. The 28th was the publication date.
- **D158 (Canada).** The release date, 13 November 1953, is the cover letter's date. The sighting's time group is "080315Z November 1953." <Cite card="e5c4575f838f0175" page={3} q="080315Z November 1953." /> That is the evening of 7 November in British Columbia. The release description mentions three servicemembers. The report lists two. (We do not cite D158 page 2, whose machine text does not match the document.)
- **D161 (Diomede).** The release says 6 October. The header's date of information is 8 October <Cite card="f52425adaa32c4f2" page={1} q="DATE OF INFORMATION 8 October 1953 EVALUATION C-3" />, as is the time group, while the body says "This report was made on 6 October 1953" <Cite card="f52425adaa32c4f2" page={2} q="This report was made on 6 October 1953" />. The document disagrees with itself.
- **D164 (Indiana).** The title says 1953 and the file name says 1954, the letter's year. The release gives 1 December 1953 and no location. The document gives "Seymour, Indiana." <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={3} q="Seymour, Indiana." /> For the date it says "Observers unable to remember exactly." <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={3} q="Date: Observers unable to remember exactly." /> Its weather covers "the week of 8 December to 15 December 1953." <Cite card="6f5beb626837fa74" page={4} q="the week of 8 December to 15 December 1953." /> Nothing on the pages supports 1 December.
- **Catalina.** All four Catalina file names carry the label Unresolved UAP Report, and three of the release titles do. None of the documents calls its case unresolved. Their evaluations are insufficient data, none, Jupiter and a probable meteor.

## Catalina Island, four times

**1949 (D181).** A private letter from Avalon to Kenneth Arnold reports six lights seen one at a time: "If you are still in- terested in flying disks, saucers etc., please let me know and I will write more in full." <Cite card="55f5f0db04de21f2" page={3} q="If you are still in- terested in flying disks, saucers etc., please let me know and I will write more in full." /> The pages do not say how it reached the Air Force. A routing slip is marked "FROM: 17th Distr Kirtland Fld DATED 5/20/49" <Cite card="55f5f0db04de21f2" page={2} q="FROM: 17th Distr Kirtland Fld DATED 5/20/49" />, and a note reads "MCIAXS did not acknowledge this ltr" <Cite card="55f5f0db04de21f2" page={3} q="MCIAXS did not acknowledge this ltr" />. The record card: "INSUFFICIENT DATA FOR EVALUATION" <Cite card="55f5f0db04de21f2" page={1} q="INSUFFICIENT DATA FOR EVALUATION" />.

**1952 (D179).** Three Boy Scouts on a hike saw a "Silver object faster than jet" <Cite card="1af7a333fa99bdd9" page={1} q="Silver object faster than jet" /> that ended in "disappearance by disintegration." The report cites AF Letter 200-5, not AFR 200-2. Its evaluation is "None" <Cite card="1af7a333fa99bdd9" page={2} q="28 August 1952 O/A 25 June 1952 None" />, and "No estimate of the reliability can be attempted by the reporter." <Cite card="1af7a333fa99bdd9" page={2} q="No estimate of the reliability can be attempted by the reporter." />

**1953 (D178).** An airman in San Pedro watched a purplish-white light over Catalina move northwest for ten minutes. The record card concludes "Astronomical (JUPITER)" <Cite card="a3a9754af3184c3b" page={1} q="Astronomical (JUPITER)" /> and notes "Movement of object does not coincide with winds aloft." <Cite card="a3a9754af3184c3b" page={1} q="Movement of object does not coincide with winds aloft." /> The teletype ends "ASTRONOMERS OBSERVATION MAY ACCOUNT FOR THE APPERANCE OF THE SUBJECT" [sic] <Cite card="a3a9754af3184c3b" page={5} q="ASTRONOMERS OBSERVATION MAY ACCOUNT FOR THE APPERANCE OF THE SUBJECT" />.

**1955 (D177).** A very bright orange light fell straight down near Avalon for two seconds. The teletype, sent "IN ACCORD WITH AFR 200-2 PAR 7D" <Cite card="b63bea0452d1d4b3" page={3} q="IN ACCORD WITH AFR 200-2 PAR 7D" /> for a visual sighting, concludes "FLIGHT PATH AND LENGTH OF TIME IN SIGHT, SUGGEST UFOB WAS PROBABLY A METEOR." <Cite card="b63bea0452d1d4b3" page={4} q="FLIGHT PATH AND LENGTH OF TIME IN SIGHT, SUGGEST UFOB WAS PROBABLY A METEOR." /> The record card says "Probable meteor." <Cite card="b63bea0452d1d4b3" page={1} q="Probable meteor." /> A second index card marks it "Possibly astronomical" <Cite card="b63bea0452d1d4b3" page={2} q="Possibly astronomical, but due to the" /> and not worth further investigation.

## Where this sits in the archive

- These are the field end of the system that [Air Intelligence Study No. 203 (1948)](/finds/air-intelligence-study-203-1948) analyzed from the top. Most were written in 1953, the year of [the Robertson Panel](/finds/robertson-panel-1953-strip-special-status).
- Ruppelt warned against loosely tying balloons to sightings in [Ruppelt's March 1952 Briefing](/finds/ruppelt-briefing-march-1952). Guam is a balloon check done carefully and left conditional. The press side is in [Blue Book's 1952 Press Posture](/finds/blue-book-press-posture-1952).
- The 1949 Catalina letter went to Arnold. His own account is [Kenneth Arnold, June 24 1947](/finds/kenneth-arnold-june-24-1947).
- Another radar case the Air Force put down to an atmospheric effect, a temperature inversion: [Oak Ridge, January 1951](/finds/oak-ridge-radar-january-1951). On labels that mislead: [What's Not Actually UAP](/finds/whats-not-uap).

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming any of these reports describes something extraordinary. The Air Force's own outcomes include a planet, a probable meteor, radar propagation, birds and a possible ship, and the most detailed case ends in a conditional. An A-1 or B-2 does not mean a confirmed object. In these cards the code grades the source and the information, and they do not define the scale. We have not filled that gap from outside sources. We list the title and date mismatches so readers cite the page, not the label. Witness names, addresses, service numbers and personal details are left out on purpose. Every quotation is verbatim from the released text, pinned to card and page. The selection and framing are ours.
