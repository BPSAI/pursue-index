## What's in the case file

FBI 62-HQ-83894 is the Bureau's general flying-disc case file. Two documents in it are editorially adjacent — both mention New Mexico recoveries, both flow through SAC-to-Director channels — but they are different documents with different evidential properties, filed nearly three years apart, in different Sections of the file. This entry walks through each on its own terms.

## Document 1 — The Wyly teletype, July 8, 1947

**Surface.** A single-page urgent teletype, time-stamped **6:17 PM**, dated **7-8-47**, from **FBI Dallas** to **Director and SAC, Cincinnati**. Subject line: **"FLYING DISC, INFORMATION CONCERNING."** Signed at the bottom **WYLY** — Percy Wyly, the Dallas SAC. Card `bcf2e688dfbc220d`, page 70 of FBI 62-HQ-83894 Section 1.

**Content.** Wyly is relaying a phone call he received the same day from an Eighth Air Force officer at Fort Worth. The teletype, as typed, names the caller as "MAJOR CURTAN":

> "MAJOR CURTAN, HEADQUARTERS EIGHTH AIR FORCE, TELEPHONICALLY ADVISED THIS OFFICE THAT AN OBJECT PURPORTING TO BE A FLYING DISC WAS RE COVERED NEAR ROSWELL, NEW MEXICO, THIS DATE." <Cite card="bcf2e688dfbc220d" page={70} q="PURPORTING TO BE A FLYING DISC" />

(Historians of the case identify the actual caller as **Major Edwin M. Kirton**, an Eighth Air Force intelligence officer. "CURTAN" is a teletype transmission artifact — a transposition consistent with how Kirton's name was rendered through the period's wire equipment. The teletype's surface preserves the artifact, and the corpus preserves the surface.)

**Geometry described.** Hexagonal. Suspended from a balloon by cable. The balloon, not the disc, is given a diameter of approximately twenty feet:

> "THE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY TWENTY FEET IN DIAMETER." <Cite card="bcf2e688dfbc220d" page={70} q="HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE" />

**The weather-balloon comparison, and its limits at time of transmission.** The teletype records Major Kirton's own statement that the object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector — and, in the same sentence, records that the Eighth Air Force's own check against Wright Field had not, as of 6:17 PM that day, confirmed that identification:

> "MAJOR CURTAN FURTHER ADVISED THAT THE OBJECT FOUND RESEMBLES A HIGH ALTITUDE WEATHER BALLOON WITH A RADAR REFLECTOR, BUT THAT TELEPHONIC CONVERSATION BETWEEN THEIR OFFICE AND WRIGHT FIELD HAD NOT SOUND BORNE OUT THIS BELIEF." <Cite card="bcf2e688dfbc220d" page={70} q="HAD NOT SOUND BORNE OUT THIS BELIEF" />

This is the methodologically interesting line on the page. At the moment of transmission, the AAF's own preferred identification was not yet confirmed by its own internal phone check.

**No bodies.** The teletype mentions no occupants, no human-shaped figures, no biological material of any kind. It describes an object and a balloon.

**Disposition.** The object and balloon are being flown to Wright Field for examination. The reason the FBI is being told at all is institutional housekeeping — press interest:

> "INFORMATION PROVIDED THIS OFFICE BECAUSE OF NATIONAL INTEREST IN CASE AND FACT THAT NATIONAL BROADCASTING COMPANY, ASSOCIATED PRESS, AND OTHERS ATTEMPTING TO BREAK STORY OF LOCATION OF DISC TODAY." <Cite card="bcf2e688dfbc220d" page={70} q="ATTEMPTING TO BREAK STORY" />

The closing line records the Bureau's posture: **"NO FURTHER INVESTIGATION BEING CONDUCTED."** <Cite card="bcf2e688dfbc220d" page={70} q="NO FURTHER INVESTIGATION BEING CONDUCTED" />

**Source chain in one sentence.** A same-day, named-officer phone call from the Eighth Air Force to the Dallas SAC, transmitted to the Director and Cincinnati within hours of the underlying event, while the AAF was still actively processing the recovery.

## Document 2 — The Hottel memo, March 22, 1950

**Surface.** A Standard Form No. 64 Office Memorandum, dated **March 22, 1950**, from **GUY HOTTEL, SAC, WASHINGTON** to **DIRECTOR, FBI**. Subject: **"FLYING SAUCERS — INFORMATION CONCERNING."** Card `b246c782b16be168`, page 68 of FBI 62-HQ-83894 Section 5.

**Content.** Hottel is relaying what was passed to one of his agents (SA R. H. Kurtzman) by a Metropolitan Police Department investigator (Karl Howe) who had heard it from an Air Force investigator. The chain is set out explicitly on the page:

> "The following information was furnished to SA R. H. KURTZMAN by KARL HOWE, Special Investigator, Sex Squad, Metropolitan Police Department: An investigator for the Air Forces stated that three so-called flying saucers had been recovered in New Mexico." <Cite card="b246c782b16be168" page={68} q="An investigator for the Air Forces" />

**Geometry described.** Three saucers, not one object. Each circular with raised centers. Each approximately fifty feet in diameter, not twenty:

> "They were described as being circular in shape with raised centers, approximately 50 feet in diameter." <Cite card="b246c782b16be168" page={68} q="approximately 50 feet in diameter" />

**Bodies.** Three per saucer, each three feet tall, in metallic-cloth garments:

> "Each one was occupied by three bodies of human shape but only 3 feet tall, dressed in metallic cloth of a very fine texture." <Cite card="b246c782b16be168" page={68} q="three bodies of human shape" />

**Proposed mechanism.** A radar-interference hypothesis is attributed to Howe's informant:

> "the saucers were found in New Mexico due to the fact that the Government has a very high-powered radar set-up in that area and it is believed the radar interferes with the controling mechanism of the saucers." <Cite card="b246c782b16be168" page={68} q="interferes with the controling mechanism" />

**The Bureau's own posture in the memo.** This is the single most important line on the page for assessing the document's evidential weight. Hottel closes the memo with a sentence in which the FBI affirmatively declines to evaluate what it has just relayed:

> "No further evaluation was attempted by SA KURTZMAN concerning the above." <Cite card="b246c782b16be168" page={68} q="No further evaluation was attempted" />

That sentence is the memo telling the reader what the memo is: a filing of a thirdhand report, by an agent who did not pursue it further. The Bureau is documenting that it heard the story, recording it in the case file, and stating on the face of the document that it did not work it.

**Source chain in one sentence.** Air Force investigator → Karl Howe (DC Metropolitan Police, Sex Squad) → SA Kurtzman → SAC Hottel → Director, with no Bureau investigative action attached at the bottom.

## What the two documents actually differ on

| Property | Wyly teletype (1947) | Hottel memo (1950) |
|---|---|---|
| Date | July 8, 1947 — same day as the Roswell incident | March 22, 1950 — nearly three years later |
| Section of 62-HQ-83894 | Section 1, page 70 | Section 5, page 68 |
| Number of objects | One | Three |
| Geometry | Hexagonal, suspended by cable from a balloon | Circular saucers, raised centers |
| Stated diameter | ~20 ft (the balloon, not the disc) | ~50 ft (each saucer) |
| Occupants mentioned | None | Three per saucer, ~3 ft tall, in metallic cloth |
| Source chain | Same-day named-officer phone call from HQ Eighth Air Force, while AAF was actively processing the event | Thirdhand: AF investigator → DC police investigator → SA → SAC |
| FBI's posture in the document | Routing fresh same-day intelligence to the Director and Cincinnati; "no further investigation being conducted" because the AAF is handling it | Filing a report at the case file, with an explicit on-page statement that "no further evaluation was attempted" |

The geometries do not match each other. The diameters do not match each other. The bodies in one are not in the other. The dates are nearly three years apart. The source chains and the Bureau's posture in each case are different.

## Why both documents are in this archive

The corpus's editorial discipline is to preserve the surface of each primary source — what the document actually says, who signed it, when, through what channel, with what disclaimers — and let the reader judge. These two documents sit adjacent in the case file but are evidence for different propositions filed under different conditions: one a same-day intelligence relay during an active recovery, the other a thirdhand filing the Bureau explicitly declined to evaluate.

A reader who clicks through to card `bcf2e688dfbc220d` can read the Wyly teletype's twelve typed lines and assess for themselves what kind of evidential object a same-day, named-officer wire relay is. A reader who clicks through to card `b246c782b16be168` can read Hottel's eleven-line memo and assess what kind of evidential object a thirdhand filing labeled "no further evaluation was attempted" is. Both judgments are available because both documents are preserved at their primary surface.
