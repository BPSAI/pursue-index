## Why a reader's guide

FBI file **62-HQ-83894** is what the FBI calls an *omnibus* file: not a single investigation but a topical bucket the Bureau used to collect everything that came in about "flying discs," "flying saucers," and "unidentified flying objects" between 1947 and 1968. PURSUE includes the file in ten parts, one per FBI section, with the FBI's own serial-number ranges preserved on the cover sheets:

Each section opens with the FBI's standard 4-series cover sheet (4-596, 4-895, 4-896, 4-564 — the form numbers are visible at the top). Most cover sheets carry the same authority statement:

> "Declassification authority derived from FBI Automatic Declassification Guide, issued May 24, 2007."[^cover-sheets]

Many serials are 1947 contemporaneous; the declassification stamp is from 2007. We are looking at material that took **sixty years** to clear inside the filing system.

## Section 1 (`bcf2e688dfbc220d`) — June–August 1947, the deluge

The file opens at the moment "flying disc" became a national news story. Section 1 is heavy on civilian correspondence, newspaper clippings (one page reproduces a full Sunday photo spread captioned *"Who, what, where, why are flying disks? The year's biggest mystery produced these Sunday photos while reports came from everywhere and everyone that the saucers were everywhere"*[^section1-clipping]), and the Bureau's first attempts to acknowledge how much of this material to take seriously.

A representative early Hoover-office memorandum forwards material to the War Department:

> "There are attached hereto copies of a letter received from the above-captioned individual, together with copies of a newspaper clipping enclosure, concerning 'flying discs.' Mr. Reibold's letter has been acknowledged and he has been advised that copies of his letter have been furnished to you for your consideration."[^section1-reibold]

The cover memo is "BY SPECIAL MESSENGER" to "Director of Intelligence, War Department General Staff, The Pentagon" dated **August 5, 1947**, six weeks after the Kenneth Arnold sighting.

Many pages in this section are heavily faded carbon copies. Approximately a third of the section's pages have OCR confidence below 50, with a number falling back to the LLM-vision pipeline that returns annotations like `[ILLEGIBLE - mostly blank page with faded stamped text at bottom]`. (See [Lowry 1949 — what bad OCR looks like](/finds/lowry-1949-ocr-limits) for a separate entry on OCR limits.)

## Section 2 (`4844321219e306af`) — August 1947 onward

Section 2 is dense with field-office teletypes. This is where you find:

- An **August 5, 1947, URGENT teletype from FBI Detroit** about a Dow Chemical Company employee who brought claimed "flying disc" debris to the company's physics laboratory.[^section2-dow]
- An anonymous **July 5, 1947 telegram** received in Washington advising that Major Paul Gaynor of AAF Headquarters consult one Richard F. Shaver of Lily Lake, Illinois, "WHO MAY HAVE UMPORTANT INFORMATION CONCERNING THEIR ORIGIN."[^section2-shaver]
- A handwritten letter from **A. Courtney Parker, Superintendent of Schools** in Strafford, Vermont, dated September 17, 1947, describing what he called "a so-called flying saucer" seen at 3:40 PM while watching honey bees on Rix Ledges north of Lebanon, New Hampshire.[^section2-vermont]
- An **August 14, 1947 internal memo** "relative to the reported air crash of two AAF officers investigating flying disc complaints," with the recommendation that the Liaison Section pass it to Air Force Intelligence.[^section2-crash]
- An **August 6, 1947 SAC San Francisco teletype** noting that "LT. COL. DONALD SPRINGER A-TWO HAMILTON FIELD, IN CHARGE OF INVESTIGATION BY A-TWO IS THOUGHT BY A-TWO TO BE RETURNING FROM KELSO, WASHINGTON AREA…"[^section2-springer] — Springer is the same officer whose 4th Air Force memo cover-letters the Muroc affidavits in Section 3.

## Section 3 (`99c12a9c49a91750`) — late 1947 – 1948, including the Muroc affidavits

This is the section that contains the [Muroc AAF sworn statements of July 8, 1947](/finds/muroc-1947) — five Air Force personnel describing two separate disc sightings hours apart at the dry-lake test base that became Edwards AFB. The full file index, witness affidavits, and the Lt. Col. Donald Springer cover memo all live in pages 60–72 of this section.

The Muroc material is not unique to Section 3 — the same incident also appears as **Incident #1** in the Air Force's separately-organized check-list reports (see [DOW Box 7 incident summaries](/finds/dow-incidents-1-100)) — but the FBI version preserves the original sworn-affidavit form, in which the witnesses' words are first-person legal testimony rather than a standardized intake form's bullet points.

## Section 4 (`5ecebc7c28a8d8c7`) — 1948–1949, Project SIGN era

Section 4 covers the period when the Air Force's first formal UFO study, **Project SIGN** (later renamed GRUDGE), was active at Wright-Patterson. A representative page is the FBI Laboratory's worksheet for an examination conducted **September 22, 1948** at the request of the Commanding General, Air Materiel Command, of soil samples submitted under "Project 'SIGH'":

> "Examination requested: Micro (Petrographic-Geologic). Examination by: JEVOUS. Result of Examination: Returned 10/18/48 Reg mail."[^section4-sign]

(The "SIGH" spelling appears to be a contemporaneous typist error for "SIGN.")

This section also contains the file's **April 27, 1948 United Press dispatch** datelined Dayton, Ohio, headlined *"new Data, Ideas About Flying Saucers And Death of Pilot Who Chased One,"* which is the contemporary press treatment of the **Mantell incident** — Captain Thomas Mantell's January 7, 1948 fatal crash near Fort Knox while pursuing a sighted object.[^section4-mantell]

## Section 5 (`b246c782b16be168`) — 1949

Section 5 has the **July 12, 1949 Tolson-to-Director memorandum** that captures the Bureau's stance toward independent UFO informants:

> "You inquired as to what was being done with reference to the information concerning flying saucers, which information was furnished by Ernest Cuneo. […] a letter is being prepared for the Los Angeles Office suggesting that they make a discreet check of the background of the individual who wrote the letter to Winchell and thereafter interview him for details."[^section5-cuneo]

The same section contains an **OSI Serial 38-49 dated 23 June 1949**, "Subject: Unidentified Flying Objects - Report Of," which gives the kind of latitude/longitude precision that distinguishes military reports from civilian ones:

> "Location of objects when first observed: 42°38′ N, 119°43′ W. Location of objects when last seen: 42°28′ N, 119°48′ W."[^section5-osi]

## Section 6 (`13f86e95aed52840`) — 1949–1952, including formal reporting policy

Section 6 includes a key piece of correspondence: the **letter from Major General C. P. Cabell**, Department of the Air Force Headquarters, to Hon. J. Edgar Hoover transmitting the Air Force's instructions for "Reporting of Information on Unconventional Aircraft":

> "The inclosed letter, subject: 'Reporting of Information on Unconventional Aircraft,' is forwarded for your information and any cooperation you may be able to extend. As indicated in the letter it is desired to place this subject in its proper perspective as part of the normal USAF requirement for information of air technical intelligence interest."[^section6-cabell]

It also contains a multi-page **17th District OSI summary table** of "Sightings of Unknown Aerial Phenomena" — a tabular log of 1949–1950 observations across Sandia Base, Roswell, Albuquerque, Los Alamos, Alamogordo, and other New Mexico locations, columns for *Date, Time, Number Observed, Apparent Direction of Flight, Color, Shape, Manner of Disappearance, Reliability*, and so on.[^section6-osi-table] The section continues through the **February 10, 1951 Knoxville report** on "UNIDENTIFIED OBJECTS OVER OAK RIDGE AREA, PROTECTION OF VITAL INSTALLATIONS"[^section6-oakridge] and Hoover's August 8, 1952 referral to the Air Force of a letter from one Florence Whitish of Charleston, South Carolina:

> "Miss Whitish's communication has been acknowledged and she has been informed of this referral. No investigation is contemplated with regard to this matter."[^section6-whitish]

This sentence-pattern — *acknowledged, referred to Air Force, no FBI investigation* — recurs in the file with such regularity that it becomes the practical answer to the question the file's existence raises.

## Section 7 (`f85532f0514320be`) — 1952–1954, the saucer-letter era

Section 7 is dominated by **citizen letters to the Director, FBI** — handwritten and typed — from members of the public proposing methods to catch, study, communicate with, or surrender to flying saucers. A representative example is the **January 24, 1953 letter from Los Angeles** addressed to Hoover:

> "This is being written to you so that it may be forwarded to President Eisenhower if you feel it advisable; and to prevent any security leaks if such transmission of message is to be accomplished. PURPOSE — A PLAN FOR BRINGING DOWN ONE OF THE STRANGE AIR VEHICLES AS SIGHTED OVER JAPAN AND ELSEWHERE IN THE WORLD; AND THEN TO BE STUDIED BY OUR SCIENTISTS."[^section7-plan]

A later page in the same section contains a **June 18, 1954 SAC Seattle teletype** documenting the Bureau's working understanding of how the Air Force ran the matter internally:

> "OSI advises us that they have no interest whatsoever themselves in connection with these matters and that Operations of the Air Force doesn't even advise them. Colonel S. W. RAYNOR, District Commander, OSI, states that there is an evaluation at Wright-Patterson Field where the data is correlated, that the reports are handled from the Air Force here to that point in a routine fashion and that OSI gives no credence whatsoever to it because they have never had any previous experience of any kind as to anything arising out of the situation."[^section7-osi]

## Section 8 (`ad721de274bb7d6c`) — 1954–1957

Section 8 contains a **July 28, 1955 Cincinnati press clipping** preserved in the file, headlined *"See 'Em? --- Saucers Cover City"* — three discs reportedly sighted in formation over Cincinnati, with the additional reportage that "Stringfield said Cincinnati is supposed to be a magnetic fault line and that perhaps the saucers come here to recharge."[^section8-cincinnati] (Leonard Stringfield was a prominent civilian UFO researcher of the period; the press clip is preserved without Bureau commentary.)

The same section has the **April 20, 1955 letter from Air Force Headquarters** to Hoover regarding one Charles A. Yost of the OC Research Laboratory, St. Clair Shores, Michigan, forwarded by the Director of Special Investigations, Alaskan Air Command.[^section8-yost] It closes with a **November 8, 1957 SAC Houston teletype** about an object "BETWEEN SIZE OF GOLF BALL AND BASKETBALL" found in the yard of a Houston resident's home.[^section8-houston]

## Section 9 (`8bfd94484138d59b`) — 1958–1963

Section 9 contains an internal-policy memorandum *"Memorandum to Mr. Donahoe, RE: FLYING SAUCERS"* that lays out the Bureau's actual standing arrangement:

> "Neither Agents Handbook nor manuals contain instructions regarding handling of reports of sightings of unidentified flying objects although policy has been well established by above Bureau bulletins and SAC Letters and no problems have arisen in connection with this matter. Air Force has on several occasions assured that Bureau will be advised of any developments of interest to us. Last such assurance given 11-12-57 (62-83894-387). Our liaison with Air Force on this matter is with OSI although within Air Force this matter is handled by Air Technical and Intelligence Center, Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio."[^section9-donahoe]

The section also contains the **George W. Van Tassel investigative file** — a multi-page report on the founder of the Giant Rock contactee gatherings:

> "they have the power to know your thoughts just as JESUS did. He said this is their means of communication and many of the space people are mute, but they train a certain number of them to speak earth languages."[^section9-vantassel]

The Bureau's interest in Van Tassel is documented as an investigative subject; the document does not draw conclusions about the truthfulness of his claims.

The section closes with **Hoover's July 13, 1964 form letter** to one Hayden Hewes of the "Interplanetary Intelligence of Unidentified Flying Objects" in Oklahoma City:

> "It has been the FBI's long-standing policy not to conduct investigations regarding information this Bureau receives about unidentified flying objects since such matters are not within our jurisdiction. All such data is immediately sent to the Department of the Air Force for its consideration."[^section9-hoover-policy]

This is the Bureau's stock answer for the entire 1960s.

## Section 10 (`7d58f0cac741650a`) — 1963–1968, the wind-down

Section 10 is thin compared to the earlier sections. It contains **convention programs from civilian flying-saucer conferences** ("COMING 1966 FLYING SAUCER CONVENTIONS")[^section10-convention], a **October 10, 1967 Dallas memorandum** about an anonymous informant routed to OSI Tinker AFB[^section10-dallas], and an extended citizen letter combining UFO claims with Cold War warnings ("Russia is well aware of this fact and has capitalized upon this fact. They have in this way been able to penetrate our Radar System undetected.")[^section10-russia].

A second Hoover policy form letter appears in this section, dated after his prior communication had been "destroyed":

> "[my position on UFOs] has not changed since I wrote to you on January 26, 1965. Also, it is contrary to my long-standing policy to express an opinion on such matters. Since my prior communication to you has been destroyed, I am enclosing another for your files…"[^section10-hoover-2]

After 1968, the file goes quiet. Project Blue Book closed in 1969, and the FBI's omnibus UFO file did not re-open as such.

## What the file is, and what it isn't

This file is **the FBI's correspondence and field-office traffic on the subject**, not an FBI investigation of UFO reality. The Bureau's posture is consistent across two decades: receive, acknowledge, refer to the Air Force, do not investigate. Field offices forward; the Director's office signs the form letter; the matter goes to OSI or to ATIC at Wright-Patterson.

The file *contains* primary-source UFO testimony — most notably the [Muroc affidavits](/finds/muroc-1947) preserved in Section 3 — but the file is not those affidavits. It is the entire administrative record around how the Bureau decided to handle them, which is by **passing them on**.

For a journalist or researcher: if you want the witness statements, go directly to the section that contains them (the Muroc affidavits are in Section 3, pages 60–72; the William Albert Rhodes Phoenix sighting affidavit material is referenced through both this file and the parallel DOW Box 7 incident summaries). If you want to understand FBI policy toward the topic, read Hoover's 1964 form letter in Section 9 first; everything else is variation on its theme.
