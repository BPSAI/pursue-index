## What the document is

Card `fd342b7508668b0e` is **"ODNI-UAP-D001, USPER Narrative, Senior USIC Official"** — a two-page first-person narrative carrying the Office of the Director of National Intelligence as releasing agency. The manifest characterizes it as "a first-hand account written by a currently serving (May 2026) senior U.S. intelligence official." The release date on the index is 2026-05-22 (Release 02); the incident date the document recounts is `Late 2025`, in the `Western United States`. The manifest records `redacted: false` — a flag that the rest of this entry treats as load-bearing.

The narrative opens in the first-person voice:

> "In late 2025, during early evening daylight hours, I — a senior U.S. intelligence officer — along with a colleague and two pilots, departed our Joint Operations Center (JOC) in a helicopter." <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={1} q="senior U.S. intelligence officer" />

This is the same person whose account is summarized, in third-person and with every proper noun redacted, in card `be533a4ab886eb11` — the **FBI FD-302** already documented in this archive at [/finds/fbi-usper-2025-orb](/finds/fbi-usper-2025-super-hot-orb). The manifest's `pdf_pairing` field on `fd342b7508668b0e` lists, explicitly, "USPER Statement about UAP Sighting" alongside thirty-two FBI photographs (A001–A008, B001–B024). The pairing is declared by the corpus, not inferred.

## What the first-person grammar commits to

The FD-302 anonymizes by category — `[WITNESS 1 (a senior US intelligence official)]`, `[STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION]`, `[FEDERAL PARTNER 4]`, `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]`, `[CALL SIGN 1]`, `[DATE]`, `[BEARING]`, `[COORDINATES]`. The ODNI narrative carries none of those brackets. What it does *not* fill them in with is also part of how to read it.

The narrative refers to:

- **"I — a senior U.S. intelligence officer."** No agency, no service. The role descriptor matches the FD-302's `[WITNESS 1 (a senior US intelligence official)]` exactly. Which intelligence-community component the author sits inside is not stated. The redactor's category in the FD-302 was preserved as a self-description in the ODNI release.
- **"A colleague and two pilots."** The FD-302's `[WITNESS 2 (a senior US intelligence official)]` and `[STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION]`-employed aircrew. The ODNI text does not name them either.
- **"Our Joint Operations Center (JOC)."** Named by acronym. The FD-302 has this as `[OPERATIONS CENTER]`. The narrative also references "the test range," "the mountain range," "a prearranged rendezvous point," "a prepositioned tanker on the range," and "base" — terrain by function, not by toponym.
- **"Fighter jets… launched on a training mission in our operating area."** The FD-302's `five [MILITARY AIRCRAFT] from [REDACTED]`. The ODNI text says "fighter jets" by class but neither names the platform nor the operator, and gives a count only later ("the fighter jets") without restating "five."

What the narrative *does* fill in that the FD-302 did not: the **time of day in absolute terms** ("early evening daylight hours" on departure; "the sun had set" before the radar hits; "After landing… I then entered the JOC for a quick debrief before driving home"), the **navigation profile** ("a low-altitude 'map of the earth'[^nap] route through the mountain range"), the **rationale for descent** ("Multiple times, we spotted debris on the ground and descended for closer inspection. Each time, we determined it was remnants from rockets and other projectiles that had crashed during years of weapons testing on the range"), and the **terrain feature that occasioned an orbit** ("we discovered a large cave entrance with no visible end in sight. The terrain around the entrance offered no safe landing spot, so I instructed the pilot to orbit it several times for observation"). <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={1} q="large cave entrance" />

The FD-302 has none of these. They are details a first-person narrator volunteers; a third-person interview summary, written to a different purpose, does not.

## The two grammars on the same observations

The FD-302 records the LP/OP-relayed FLIR observation as `"super-hot,"` in quotation marks, attributed to a different federal partner. The ODNI narrative records the same observation from the helicopter perspective, in first-person, and quotes the LP/OP's words back:

> "En route, ground teams reported spotting a UAP on FLIR, describing it as 'super-hot,' low to the ground, and moving east then south at high speed. The object then split into two and changed direction." <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={1} q="super-hot" />

The same quotation marks around `"super-hot"`; the same attribution to a ground team on FLIR. The FD-302 captures the relay as a transcription; the ODNI text captures it as something the narrator *heard over the radio* and recounts. Both grammars agree on the relayed phrase.

The FD-302's 20-mile leg — `"LP/OP reported the orb gained elevation, came within ten feet of [CALL SIGN 1] and then headed east to [REDACTED]"` — is the ODNI narrative's:

> "The ground team suddenly radioed that the object had risen from the ground, approached within ten feet of the helicopter, dropped below us, and then sped away. The pilots observed it through NVGs and saw it split into two as a smaller object emerged before it accelerated out of sight. We briefly pursued but broke off, unable to match its speed." <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={1} q="ten feet of the helicopter" />

Same ten-foot proximity, same break-off-from-pursuit, same speed-mismatch conclusion. The narrative resolves an ambiguity the FD-302 carried: the "ten feet" event was *radioed in by a ground team*, not observed by the narrator's own platform. ("The ground team suddenly radioed that the object had risen from the ground…") That matches the FD-302's structural attribution — the ten-foot proximity was an LP/OP-reported event, not a sensor observation from the helicopter itself — and the first-person voice makes the relay explicit.

## The "T" formation, in the narrator's own words

The FD-302 records the recurring observation language as `"flared up one at a time in a horizontal formation."` The ODNI narrative is more specific about one instance:

> "Through NVGx, the pilots and I (using the naked eye) observed two large orbs flare up side by side, close to the helicopter — stationary and just above the rotor disk to our right. They were oval-shaped, orange with a white or yellow center, and emitted light in all directions. After a few seconds, a third orb flared up below the pair, followed by a fourth below that, forming a total of four or five in a 'T' formation under the original two. Moments later, they dimmed in reverse order, remaining stationary until they vanished from view. The entire event lasted 10-15 seconds." <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={2} q="T" />

The geometric description ("oval-shaped, orange with a white or yellow center"), the spatial-relative language ("just above the rotor disk to our right"), the formation count ("a total of four or five"), and the explicit pattern name ("'T' formation") are the narrator's own. The FD-302 captures the *grammar* of the recurring observation — flare-up, formation, sequential dim-down, 10-15 seconds — but not the formation-shape names. A reader can put the FD-302's structural-time-series treatment alongside the ODNI's geometric-prose treatment and see two different ways of writing down the same observed event.

## The chase, as observed from the helicopter

The closing paragraph of page 2 describes the fighter-jet portion of the timeline in the narrator's voice:

> "We then spotted the fighter jets entering visual range at about 23,00 feet AGL, identifiable by their blinking navigation lights. As watched from afar, the same type of orbs appeared directly above the fighters. They flared up one at a time in a horizontal formation, matching the jets' speed and flight path. After 10-15 seconds, they dimmed sequentially and disappeared. This repeated several times as the jets transited the airspace and eventually landed. I remarked to the pilots that it seemed the same orbs we had encountered were now 'chasing' the fighters." <Cite card="fd342b7508668b0e" page={2} q="chasing" />

The FD-302 records the same interpretation in its closing-comments section as the witness's words: "The orbs appeared to break off from [CALL SIGN 1] and pursue the [MILITARY AIRCRAFT]." The ODNI narrative shows where that interpretation came from — a remark the narrator made aloud to the pilots, in the moment, with the word "chasing" placed in scare-quotes by the narrator himself. The phrasing is preserved across both documents at almost identical specificity.

## What the unredacted narrative still does not say

`redacted: false` in the manifest is true at the bracket level — there are no `[REDACTED]` tokens in the OCR text. It is not the same as "fully specified." The ODNI narrative declines to name:

- **The narrator.** A senior US intelligence officer who, per the manifest description, was "currently serving (May 2026)" at the time of release. The narrative is a first-person account whose first-person voice does not include an introduction.
- **The agency or service.** "Senior U.S. intelligence officer" is the only role descriptor used. The intelligence community spans civilian and uniformed components; the narrative does not say which side the narrator sits on.
- **The colleague.** Referred to throughout as "my colleague." The FD-302's `[WITNESS 2 (a senior US intelligence official)]`.
- **The pilots' operator.** "Two pilots" who switched to "Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) and Night Vision Goggles (NVG)" at sunset. The FD-302 had this as `[STATE PARTNER ORGANIZATION]`. The ODNI narrative does not specify whether the pilots were military or civilian, federal or state.
- **The facility.** The manifest field `incident_location` reads `Western United States`; the narrative uses functional terms ("the test range," "the JOC," "base") rather than a named installation.
- **The date inside late 2025.** The opening phrase is "In late 2025." The FD-302's Zulu timestamps (1700, 1751, 2050, 2052, …, 2320) are absent from the ODNI text, which uses relative phrases ("After a brief search… After this encounter… Minutes later… After a few seconds").
- **The fighter platform.** "Fighter jets" by class only; the FD-302's `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]` with a count of five. The ODNI narrative does not restate the count.

A reader holding both documents alongside each other can recover more of the operational picture than either supports alone — but a reader holding only one of them should hold its level of specificity at exactly where the document drew it.

## The 24-photo evidence trail

The manifest's `pdf_pairing` field on `fd342b7508668b0e` lists thirty-two FBI photographs by ID — eight A-series (A001–A008) and twenty-four B-series (B001–B024) — alongside the USPER Statement. The A-series and B-series are continuous numerical sequences that the corpus delivered together with this narrative. One of them, **FBI Photo B7** (`ccd932b4f40e8de7`), is the grayscale FLIR-style frame already documented in [/finds/fbi-usper-2025-orb](/finds/fbi-usper-2025-super-hot-orb): a helicopter under a central crosshair reticle with 5/10/15 tick scales, OCR yielding only a corrupted system timestamp. The companion entry's reading of B7 — visually consistent with an LP/OP FLIR display imaging an in-air helicopter, with the manifest-described "second, smaller, dark circular object… visible below the reticle" — applies here without modification. The corpus now declares that frame to be paired with the present narrative; the editorial reading does not need to assert it.

The remaining thirty-one frames in the A/B sequence are released alongside the narrative as a corpus-declared evidence package. This entry surfaces the existence of that pairing rather than re-deriving each frame's reading; per-frame examination of all 32 photographs — including the sensor-system classification that splits the A-series from the B-series, the burst structure of the 24 B-frames, and what the imagery does and does not corroborate against this narrative — is in the companion finds entry at [/finds/helicopter-case-2025-imagery](/finds/helicopter-case-2025-imagery).

## Three grammars, one incident

With the ODNI narrative now on the index, the late-2025 helicopter-orb encounter is documented in this archive through three distinct document grammars:

1. **First-person narrative (ODNI, card `fd342b7508668b0e`)** — the participant's own account, written in prose, two pages, no agency-internal redaction. Names sensors, geometry, terrain function, and the in-the-moment word "chasing."
2. **Third-person interview summary (FBI FD-302, card `be533a4ab886eb11`)** — the agent's transcription of a witness debrief, three pages, SECRET//NOFORN, every proper noun in a redaction category. Names timestamps, personnel counts, sensor types, and the LP/OP's relayed `"super-hot."`
3. **Imagery (FBI Photo A001–A008, B001–B024 — thirty-two cards)** — ground-team FLIR frames captured during the same exercise per the manifest pairing. Per-frame direct examination of all 32 photographs — including the sensor-system classification that splits the A-series from the B-series, the burst structure of the 24 B-frames, and the operator-mediated mid-burst zoom-in — is in [/finds/helicopter-case-2025-imagery](/finds/helicopter-case-2025-imagery).

The three grammars do not perfectly overlap. The FD-302's Zulu timestamps are not in the ODNI narrative; the ODNI narrative's "large cave entrance" is not in the FD-302; the photographs' per-frame embedded data is not in either document. They are useful as three readings of the same event because each grammar commits to a different set of details.
