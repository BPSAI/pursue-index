## What the document is

Card `13f86e95aed52840` is **Section 6 of FBI file 62-HQ-83894**, the Bureau's omnibus UFO file. The full file family runs Sections 1 through 10 — approximately 1,900 pages of catalogued correspondence, investigation memoranda, citizen letters, and inter-bureau coordination between 1947 and 1968. The reading guide at [/finds/fbi-62-hq-83894-readers-guide](/finds/fbi-62-hq-83894-readers-guide) walks the section structure.

Section 6 is 271 pages, weighted heavily to 1949–1952 (year-frequency in the OCR: 1952 × 192, 1950 × 114, 1949 × 40, 1951 × 30, with smaller counts before and after). The CSV description that originally accompanied this card on war.gov characterized this version as a "complete case file with several newly declassified pages and only minor redactions" — explicitly comparing it favorably against the more-redacted version available on the FBI Vault. The prior manifest record carried `redacted: false`.

## The pull, as the new CSV states it

A direct field-by-field diff of the 2026-05-08 CSV against the 2026-05-11 CSV is unambiguous about what happened. The Section 6 title is still in the upstream listing — same description text, same "Type: PDF," same agency. The single field that changed:

> `PDF | Image Link: N/A` <Cite card="9c86c04b5e4a50e8" page={1} q="N/A" />

In the 2026-05-08 row, that field held `https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release_1/65_hs1-834228961_62-hq-83894_section_6.pdf`. In the 2026-05-11 row, the link is the string "N/A." The PDF is no longer downloadable from war.gov. Sections 1–5 and 7–10 of 62-HQ-83894 remain available. Section 6 is the only one of the ten where the asset link has been switched to N/A.

Because our `card_id` is content-addressed by `sha256(asset_url || title)[:16]`, swapping the URL produces a new card_id (`9c86c04b5e4a50e8`) at the same title — the upstream listing remains, but our manifest now carries two rows for "Section 6": the old one (`13f86e95aed52840`, with the PDF link our R2 mirror is keyed to) and the new one (`9c86c04b5e4a50e8`, with `asset_url: null`). The PDF remains servable at `/pdf/13f86e95aed52840.pdf` from this archive — we do not delete bytes when upstream changes a listing.

A snapshot of the prior manifest is preserved at `/data/snapshots/596cc1881aa97d2fa49a45edab14d60802616e73ce125d286120e00d967cafa2.json`; the change is logged at `/data/removed-cards.json` and surfaced on [/removed](/removed).

## What's in Section 6

Sampling broadly across the 271 pages, the section is organized chronologically by FBI serial number and covers six recurring threads.

### New Mexico green-fireball file (1949–1952)

The dominant thread. OSI-Kirtland 17th District summaries, communications with Dr. Lincoln LaPaz at the UNM Institute of Meteoritics, and material on the Land-Air Inc. observational contract. <Cite card="13f86e95aed52840" page={47} q="Land-Air" /> LaPaz's hypothesis that some discs could be Soviet missile tests originating in the Urals appears in correspondence on these pages.

### Sensitive-installation sightings (1949–1951)

Reports tied to Los Alamos, Sandia Base, Oak Ridge, and Hanford. The Oak Ridge material includes a January 1951 radar-track sequence at the S-50 area observed by named senior officers across three services — covered in detail in [/finds/oak-ridge-radar-january-1951](/finds/oak-ridge-radar-january-1951), which cites this card.

### The William A. Rhodes / Phoenix 1947 photographs chain of custody

Pages 170–185 walk the route of the original Rhodes negatives — FBI to OSI to a Drew Pearson television broadcast, and a contemporaneous claim by Rhodes that the Bureau "borrowed" the negatives and never returned them. The Bureau's internal characterization of the transfer is that it was extended "only as a courtesy to Air Force." <Cite card="13f86e95aed52840" page={170} q="only as a courtesy" /> The Rhodes case is the subject of a separate find: [/finds/rhodes-photographs-phoenix-1947](/finds/rhodes-photographs-phoenix-1947).

### A background investigation of a named civilian witness

Pages 156–164 are an FBI Baltimore Field Office background investigation of a named civilian who had reported a UFO sighting. The investigation includes the witness's full street address, telephone exchange, age, employer, and interviews with named neighbors at named addresses — the standard surface of a Bureau background check, applied to a citizen whose primary contact with the Bureau was reporting a sighting. The investigation also notes a 1948 questioning of the witness over a hammer-and-sickle decal on a company truck, included in the same case file.

### Citizen-letter intake

Approximately twenty letters from members of the public mentioning saucers, lights, or disc-shaped objects, each filed with the writer's name and full street address. The letters span Virginia, West Virginia, South Carolina, Indiana, and a Toronto correspondent. The Bureau's standard response — Hoover's form letter declining to confirm or deny investigative interest — accompanies several.

### Administrative correspondence

Hoover, Cabell, Belmont, Ladd, and Tolson appear throughout the inter-bureau routing — the standard 1950s FBI executive layer.

## What's NOT in Section 6

Not present in the OCR pass: no Roswell-as-crash material (the four Roswell hits in this section are routine sighting tabulations from Kirtland correspondence, not 1947 debris-recovery content), no Aztec, no Robertson Panel deliberations, no MJ-12-style claims, no living political figures.
