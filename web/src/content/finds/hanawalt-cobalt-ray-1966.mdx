## What the document is

Card `7d58f0cac741650a` is **FBI File 62-HQ-83894 Section 10** — one of ten sections of the FBI's omnibus UFO file. Sections 1–10 span 1947 to 1968 and run to roughly 1,900 pages of catalogued correspondence, investigation memoranda, sworn affidavits, news clippings, and citizen letters. The Bureau's interest, for purposes of this file, was that the contents *mentioned* UFOs — not that the contents were corroborated.

Pages 53–56 of Section 10 are a single contained document: a **January 30, 1967 memorandum from the FBI's Mexico City field office to the Director, FBI**, with the subject line "INFORMATION CONCERNING W. R. HANAWALT." The memorandum reproduces verbatim the text of a telegram that W. R. Hanawalt of Harlingen, Texas sent to the Federal Security Police of Mexico, D.F., on December 20, 1966. <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={53} q="INFORMATION CONCERNING" />

The Mexico City office's role here is courier. They received the telegram (presumably forwarded by Mexican authorities or captured in the international wire), reproduced its contents for the FBI's UFO files, and added one operational sentence at the end:

> "No information was located in the indices of the Mexico City Office identifiable with the Subject." <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={56} q="No information was located" />

That sentence is the entire editorial position of the document. Everything between the header and the closing note is Hanawalt's words, set off in quotation marks by the field office, not the Bureau's.

## What Hanawalt's telegram actually claims

The telegram is a long single-author document characteristic of the "letter to authorities" genre — wide-ranging, paranoid in register, simultaneously specific and unfalsifiable. The central claim:

> "The laser ray, or cobalt ray is a self enthroning ray, and is similar in use to a cocoon around a silk worm. The entire body is enclosed and the entire nervous system can be manipulated to a lesser or greater degree of proficiency than is normal. Visions of flying objects are merely faulty vision — fully controlled and is very real to that individual experiencing the phenomena." <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={53} q="cobalt ray" />

Hanawalt's enumeration of what the ray controls runs through breathing, heartbeat, lie-detector results, smell, taste, hearing, sight, sexual climax, mathematics, typing, machinery, ignition systems, and the monitoring of thoughts via "electric or telephone lines." <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={54} q="Any smell, taste, hearing" />

He extends the claim outward. The ray has been in use in Harlingen, Texas for eight years "by a select few in all walks of life." Most if not all national Congressmen know of the method and have used it for personal purposes in election races. The equipment is now obsolete because it has been replaced by **the satellite SAMOS** — a real US reconnaissance satellite program of the early 1960s (Satellite And Missile Observation System). <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={55} q="satellite SAMOS" /> He suspects the CIA and "space arms." He believes his driving accidents since 1946 are premeditated, because he "learned too much in the Navy, while at war."

He closes:

> "I submit myself as of sound mind and body, and that the above is true and correct of my sound opinions." <Cite card="7d58f0cac741650a" page={56} q="sound mind and body" />

There is no fact-asserting paragraph anywhere on these four pages signed by an FBI agent or any other US government party. Every claim about a cobalt ray comes from Hanawalt, in quotation marks, inside a memo whose subject line is "INFORMATION CONCERNING" him — not concerning the ray.

## Why this card is in the FBI's UFO file at all

The Bureau's interest in the Hanawalt telegram is *that it mentions UFOs* — Hanawalt writes that visions of flying objects are "merely faulty vision" produced by the ray, and elsewhere notes that he "saw unidentified flying objects in 1945 on combat missions." That mention is enough for the file. The Bureau's omnibus UFO catalogue did not distinguish, at intake, between sworn affidavits and citizen letters; both went in. The Bureau's record of having received the document does not constitute investigation of the document, and in this case the explicit "no information located" sentence on page 56 documents the opposite.

The case status notation, the absence of any FBI follow-up activity in the file, and the courier framing of the memo together place the Hanawalt telegram in the same category as a great many other letters in the omnibus file: catalogued, not investigated.

## What the document does not say

The document does **not** say that a cobalt ray exists.

The document does **not** say that the FBI investigated the claim and found evidence supporting it.

The document does **not** say that the FBI investigated the claim and found evidence against it.

The document says: a man named W. R. Hanawalt wrote a telegram from Harlingen to Mexico City on December 20, 1966, and the contents of that telegram were these. The Mexico City office of the FBI added one sentence noting they had no record of him.

## Why this card is in this archive

The Hanawalt telegram is exactly the kind of document a public, citable archive of declassified government UFO files needs to surface carefully. Treating "the FBI documented this" as "the FBI states this" collapses three distinct evidentiary layers — Bureau, courier office, citizen — and turns a 1966 letter into a self-endorsement.

The discipline this archive applies is the discipline a careful journalist or researcher would apply: read the document type before the document content; separate what the document *is* from what the document *quotes*; and apply the same abstention bar to a citizen claim that the originating agency itself applied. The FBI's Mexico City field office did not endorse the cobalt ray. Neither does this archive.

The telegram is a primary source. So is the disclaimer at the end of the memo.
