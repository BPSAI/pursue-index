## What the documents are

- **DOW-UAP-D174** (`d6016299267e9604`): *Utah Ranch Investigation Report, April 2010*, 60 pages. It is the main source for this entry. It has a cover sheet, an executive summary, an abstract, interview write-ups, a section on plant experiments and an appendix. The appendix holds a camera list, microscope photographs, an outside lab's letter, a BAASS email interpreting that letter, and witness spreadsheets dated 5/7/2010.
- **DOW-UAP-D173** (`ffea128d68063d60`): *BAASS Monthly Report, November 2009*, 155 pages. It is used here for what BAASS was doing at the ranch before the spring trips.
- **DOW-UAP-D169** (`85fd315af4b25993`): *BAASS Monthly Report, March 2009*, 42 pages. It is used for one line in BAASS's staffing chart.

D174 itself never names the Defense Intelligence Agency or AAWSAP. Its place in that program comes from BAASS's 2009 monthly reports. D169 lists Utah ranch security officers on its staff chart, and D173 reports the ranch surveillance and refers to "the sponsor" without naming it. BAASS's 2010 capabilities summary says DIA's Defense Warning Office contracted it for 2008 to 2010, and the DIA order published in Release 6 calls for monthly status reports. The release also tags all three files as AAWSAP records. The monthly reports are the subject of the [BAASS contractor reports entry](/finds/baass-aawsap-contractor-reports-2008-2010).

The release manifest gives D174's incident location as Las Vegas, Nevada. That is where BAASS had its office, not where the work happened. The appendix email is signed from "Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies Las Vegas NV" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={38} q="Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies Las Vegas NV" />. The fieldwork took place in Utah.

The ranch's name is redacted throughout. Where it would appear, the release substitutes the label "AAP Person 4". This entry does not try to identify the ranch, its owner, or any witness.

## Who went, when, and what they were looking for

The cover sheet lists the deployments as "March 22 – 26, April 19 – 23, 2010" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={1} q="March 22 – 26, April 19 – 23, 2010" />. It names a Task Force Director (AAP Person 7), two investigators (AAP Person 41 and 14) and two scientists (AAP Person 29 and 28). The executive summary describes "a team of four BAASS personnel traveled" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={5} q="a team of four BAASS personnel traveled" />, made up of the two investigators and the two scientists. Its aim was to find evidence of the reported creatures and lights, re-interview witnesses, find new ones, and set up digital game cameras on the ranch and the land around it.

The abstract calls the work "an ongoing project which to date has incorporated several deployments of investigators, scientists and engineers" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={8} q="an ongoing project which to date has incorporated several deployments of investigators, scientists and engineers" />. It sets out four aims. The first is mass interviewing. The second is sensor work: magnetic fields, the EM spectrum, ionizing radiation and soil chemistry. The third is plant biosensors. The fourth is stated in these words:

> "To initiate a variety of games, puzzles, and other strategies in order to establish communication with the source/origin of reported anomalies on the ranch property." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={8} q="To initiate a variety of games, puzzles, and other strategies in order to establish communication with the source/origin of reported anomalies on the ranch property." />

D174 itself describes no such game or puzzle from these two trips. The November 2009 report does describe one approach. Experiments "are assembled in Las Vegas, photographed, locked in briefcases and secured at BAASS and then transported to the Utah ranch" <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={114} q="are assembled in Las Vegas, photographed, locked in briefcases and secured at BAASS and then transported to the Utah ranch." />. BAASS gives its reason for the locked cases in the same passage. Earlier anecdotal reports of moved coins "suffer from the obvious criticisms that they can be easily explained by (a) fraud perpetrated by the officers, (b) trespassers, (c) animals etc." <Cite card="ffea128d68063d60" page={114} q="suffer from the obvious criticisms that they can be easily explained by (a) fraud perpetrated by the officers, (b) trespassers, (c) animals etc." />

## What witnesses told them

The abstract gives the interview count:

> "To date, May 7, 2010, over twenty-five (25) people have been contacted and interviewed in person, predominantly at their homes." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={8} q="To date, May 7, 2010, over twenty-five (25) people have been contacted and interviewed in person, predominantly at their homes." />

The interviewees included local police officers, ranchers and business owners. The report summarizes what they described: "Witnesses have reported unidentified balls of light at close range, daytime sightings of" two-legged dog-like creatures, along with howling, "little people", and large birds and worms <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={8} q="Witnesses have reported unidentified balls of light at close range, daytime sightings of" />. The bipedal canine accounts are the largest group. The report counts them as "Total number of independent witnesses (separately interviewed) eleven." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={9} q="Total number of independent witnesses (separately interviewed) eleven." /> It keeps a hedge on the timing: "the vast majority of reports detailed in points 1-3 have allegedly occurred within the last five to seven years" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={9} q="the vast majority of reports detailed in points 1-3 have allegedly occurred within the last five to seven years." />.

The interview pages and the appendix spreadsheets give these accounts in detail: heights, fur color, odor, sound, and how the lights moved. This entry does not repeat them. They are what private people told BAASS investigators, written down by those investigators. BAASS did not observe any of it.

Two methods sections are worth noting. The first is a map. BAASS plotted each creature sighting with the witness's last-seen direction of travel, extended those lines as straight paths, and marked where they crossed. The authors flagged the weakness themselves:

> "In a sense, these intersection points could be construed as arbitrary, particularly since it is recognized that the creatures may have changed their direction of travel once out of sight of the eyewitnesses." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={6} q="In a sense, these intersection points could be construed as arbitrary, particularly since it is recognized that the creatures may have changed their direction of travel once out of sight of the eyewitnesses." />

They still put forward the crossing points as candidate sites for night watches. This entry does not reproduce the map or its coordinates.

The second is a questionnaire. One witness had said a red ball of light came within inches of his face. BAASS wrote that "Biologist AAP Person 29 designed questions to aid in the identification of the type of energy emanating from the red ball of light by focusing on physical effects." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={15} q="Biologist AAP Person 29 designed questions to aid in the identification of the type of energy emanating from the red ball of light by focusing on physical effects." /> The questions cover smell, sound, heat, numbness, eyesight, headache and sleep. In general terms, the answers describe brief sensations at the time and no lasting effects. The report notes that during the interview the witness "was unable to give his full attention" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={15} q="was unable to give his full attention." />.

## The instruments

**EM sweep.** The summary reports one reading taken on the mesa at the ranch:

> "One set of measurements taken at a point on the mesa at the ranch indicated a strong, yet to be explained, signal which was not detected on previous readings." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={9} q="One set of measurements taken at a point on the mesa at the ranch indicated a strong, yet to be explained, signal which was not detected on previous readings." />

It quotes an internal write-up giving two clusters of peaks, at 1210 to 1216 MHz and at "1232.5, 1235.5 and 1238.5 MHz" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={10} q="the second large spike was 1232.5, 1235.5 and 1238.5 MHz." />. The same passage places them in allocated spectrum: "The signals fell in the frequency bands of 1164-1215 and 1215-1240 MHz." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={10} q="The signals fell in the frequency bands of 1164-1215 and 1215-1240 MHz." /> The allocations it lists include Earth exploration-satellite, radiolocation, radionavigation-satellite and space research services, and it notes that the bands are primarily for military use. Its conclusion is a to-do:

> "Further measurements and research, particularly into satellite transmitting frequencies and times will be required to identify the origin of these signals." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={10} q="Further measurements and research, particularly into satellite transmitting frequencies and times will be required to identify the origin of these signals." />

The report calls the signal "yet to be explained". That is as far as this entry goes. The report's own frequency list points first to satellite services, and D174 records no follow-up measurement.

**Game cameras.** Seven cameras went up on the ranch and on a neighboring property. Custody was set out as a procedure: "A protocol was also established to have the memory cards of the cameras secured on a weekly basis by BAASS security personnel" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={5} q="A protocol was also established to have the memory cards of the cameras secured on a weekly basis by BAASS security personnel" />. The appendix lists nine camera IDs and marks four as no longer in service. The report presents no camera image or result.

**Plants.** Six spiderwort plants and six tobacco plants went to the ranch on April 6. Both are standard mutation assays. Tobacco shows color spots, and spiderwort stamen hairs change from blue to pink. The tobacco check found nothing: "inspected all Tobacco leaves for the presence of dark green spots; none are present at this time." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={31} q="inspected all Tobacco leaves for the presence of dark green spots; none are present at this time." /> The spiderwort check had not been done: "The April deployment was too soon to perform microscopic analysis." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={31} q="The April deployment was too soon to perform microscopic analysis." /> A seed germination experiment began on April 22, with a control set at BAASS. No results are reported.

## The hair samples

During the March trip, a witness told the team that a creature chased by his dogs had run into his barbed-wire fence two nights earlier. An investigator searched the fence line and found fur caught on a barb. The report says: "This sample was collected and submitted for scientific analysis." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={17} q="This sample was collected and submitted for scientific analysis." />

The appendix has the outcome. An outside laboratory, whose name is redacted and which this entry does not try to identify, wrote on April 27, 2010. Its letter carries a BAASS received stamp of May 3. The lab had received four hair samples on April 2 for mitochondrial sequencing. It reported:

> "Sample SU-01 tested as FCU188-1 matched reference Procyon lotor (raccoon) samples in GenBank." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={37} q="Sample SU-01 tested as FCU188-1 matched reference Procyon lotor (raccoon) samples in GenBank." />

The other three shared one canine sequence: "This sequence is frequently found in domestic dogs and would be expected to occur in 9.8% to 13.8% of random dogs at the 95% confidence interval." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={37} q="This sequence is frequently found in domestic dogs and would be expected to occur in 9.8% to 13.8% of random dogs at the 95% confidence interval." />

Next comes an internal BAASS email from AAP Person 18 to the Task Force Director. It matches the lab's codes to the samples. Sample 1 was hair from a red-stained rock found on the mesa in January. Sample 2 was a set of control hairs taken from one of the witness's own dogs. Samples 3 and 4 were short white and long black hairs from the fence "near where the" creature was seen. The email reads the result with its own hedge and then a firmer inference:

> "The Hypervariable 1 (HVI) DNA sequence for the last three samples were identical suggesting (but not 100% definitive) the same canine individual." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={38} q="The Hypervariable 1 (HVI) DNA sequence for the last three samples were identical suggesting (but not 100% definitive) the same canine individual." />

It cites the hairs' physical similarity and the fact that the dogs run loose on the property as strong grounds for the same-dog reading. On the red-stain hair:

> "The analysis of the DNA sequence of the hair embedded in the red stain identified the hair as originating from a raccoon." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={38} q="The analysis of the DNA sequence of the hair embedded in the red stain identified the hair as originating from a raccoon." />

It adds: "Several individuals at the ranch have reported the presence of raccoons in the vicinity of the homesteads, so this finding would not be out of the ordinary." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={38} q="Several individuals at the ranch have reported the presence of raccoons in the vicinity of the homesteads, so this finding would not be out of the ordinary." /> Its overall verdict: "The results for the samples were fairly straightforward." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={38} q="The results for the samples were fairly straightforward." />

## The report against its own appendix

The abstract is dated May 7, 2010, a few days after BAASS received the lab letter and the email. Its numbered summary still describes hair as pending. It mentions hair gathered near unexplained snow tracks on the ranch:

> "These hair samples are currently undergoing scientific DNA analysis." <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={9} q="These hair samples are currently undergoing scientific DNA analysis." />

The report does not say whether the snow-track hairs were among the four samples tested. The red-stain hair from the mesa was one of them. The fence hair from the March interview is sent to "Scientific Analysis for complete details of sample and results" <Cite card="d6016299267e9604" page={17} q="Please refer to Scientific Analysis for complete details of sample and results." />. That section covers only the plant experiments. The results are in the appendix and are not mentioned in the body. A reader who stops at the summary would come away thinking the physical evidence was still out. A reader who gets to page 37 finds a raccoon and a dog.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming that anything at the ranch was explained or unexplained beyond what the report says. The witness accounts are accounts. They are written down as private people gave them, and nothing in D174 tests them. The 1.2 GHz readings are, in the report's words, "yet to be explained". The report itself points to satellite frequencies as the next thing to check. The plant and seed experiments had no results yet. The cameras produced nothing the report shows. The one physical test that did come back found raccoon hair and domestic-dog hair, and BAASS's own email called the results straightforward. That result does not show that no witness saw anything. It shows what those four samples were. We are also not claiming the stale summary was meant to mislead. It may simply not have been updated after the appendix was added. We have withheld witness details, place names beyond the state, the ranch's identity and the report's images. Every quotation above is verbatim from the released text, pinned to its card and page. The selection and framing are ours.
