## What the document is

**DOW-UAP-D100** (`da533c72f476d84f`) is *Air Materiel Command Report on Unidentified Flying Objects, 1947–1948*, 245 pages of Project Sign records. The file mixes memoranda, indorsements and exhibits, and some pages are repeated copies of the same letter. Release 5 is the first time the corpus holds the key letters below. This entry reads the correspondence between Air Force Headquarters and Air Materiel Command, and then the pieces that show how the project worked day to day.

Only officials acting in their roles are named. The file also contains letters from private citizens with their names and addresses; this entry does not use them.

## November 1948: Headquarters asks for conclusions

On 3 November 1948 the Director of Intelligence, Major General C. P. Cabell, wrote to the Commanding General of Air Materiel Command (the letter appears on pp. 1 to 3). Its second paragraph is the line this entry is named for:

> "The conclusion appears inescapable that some type of" flying object has been observed. <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={3} q="The conclusion appears inescapable that some type of" />

It goes on that the identification and origin "of these objects is not discernible to this Headquarters", and that efforts to determine whether they are domestic or foreign must increase <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={3} q="of these objects is not discernible to this Headquarters." />. The third paragraph turns to the press. The press "is about to take it" into its own hands and demand to be told what the Air Force knows, and "Silence on our part will not long be accept-" able <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={3} q="Silence on our part will not long be accept-" />. Paragraph 4 asks for AMC's conclusions and for a recommendation on whether information should be offered to the press or withheld until it is sought <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={3} q="date and your recommendations as to the information to be given" />.

## Air Materiel Command answers

AMC's first indorsement of 8 November 1948 (pp. 7 to 9, signed for the commanding general by Colonel H. M. McCoy) is the longest statement of the project's thinking in the file. It reports a study of "approximately 180 such incidents" and sorts the objects into four shapes: discs, cigar shapes, spheres and balls of light <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={7} q="a study of approximately 180 such incidents." />. It says some were identified as balloons or as astronomical objects, such as Venus seen in daylight, and that the possibility of hallucination, optical illusion or deliberate hoax was considered <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={7} q="The possibility that some of the sightings" />. Then it says what remains. It states that "no physical evidence of the existence of the unidentified sightings has" been obtained <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={8} q="no physical evidence of the existence of the unidentified sightings has" />.

Paragraph 7 is the one a reader is likely to stop at. All the information made available to AMC "indicates that the discs, the cigar shaped objects, and the "balls of light"" are not of domestic origin <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={8} q="not of domestic origin." />. Paragraph 8 then addresses the other possibility directly. The possibility that the objects are vehicles from another planet "has not been ignored", but evidence for it is "completely lacking"; the file plots the incidents against the approaches of Mercury, Venus and Mars and finds a periodic variation, noting "but it may be purely a coincidence" <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={8} q="is noted, but it may be purely a coincidence." />. The next paragraph cites "The Books of Charles Fort" as showing that similar phenomena had been reported for a century <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={8} q="The Books of Charles Fort" />.

The conclusions (pp. 8 and 9) are carefully limited. Observers have actually seen "some type of flying object" they cannot classify as an aircraft; there is no conclusive proof the unidentified objects are real aircraft; and the exact nature of the objects cannot be established until "physical evidence, such as that which would result from a crash, has been" obtained <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={9} q="physical evidence, such as that which would result from a crash, has been" />.

On the press, AMC advises against presenting the unidentified cases. If the press insists on a statement, it suggests telling them that many objects "have been identified as weather balloons or astral bodies" <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={9} q="the objects sighted have been identified as weather balloons or astral" />.

Our reading: the two letters together show a headquarters that could not identify the objects and an analytical command that found the foreign-origin case stronger than the extraterrestrial one, and a recommended public line that mentions only the identified cases. That is our interpretation, but each part is stated in the letters.

## How Sign ran

The rest of the file is the plumbing.

**Fighters on alert.** On 12 February 1948 the Directorate of Intelligence relayed an AMC proposal that every Air Force installation in the United States and Alaska keep "a minimum of one each fighter or night interceptor type aircraft", crews and gun cameras included, ready to chase reported objects <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={194} q="a minimum of one each fighter or" />. The Director of Plans and Operations refused on 3 March 1948. Among his reasons:

> "Proper interception is not possible, except by accident, without complete" radar coverage "which the Air Force is not capable of providing" <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={140} q="Proper interception is not possible, except by accident, without complete" />

He recommended that AMC's role be limited to setting up direct channels for receiving information. Part of this exchange also appears in an earlier release; D100 holds the whole chain.

**A clipping service by Post Office box.** On 28 April 1948 Headquarters answered AMC's request for a press-clipping service for Project Sign. To avoid compromising the Air Force, it suggested that a civilian member of AMC's intelligence staff "secure a Post Office box in" Dayton and contract for the service in his own name <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={104} q="member of your Intelligence staff to secure a Post Office box in" />. The letter lists commercial clipping bureaus; this entry does not reproduce their addresses.

**A question for RAND.** An exhibit of Project Sign study requirements asks RAND for the design and performance parameters that would distinguish spaceships. It says that if such craft have been sighted, "it is believed more likely that they represent the effort of a foreign nation, rather than a product from beyond the Earth", and that any such craft would conform to established science <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={34} q="than a product from beyond the Earth." />.

**A question for the Director of Central Intelligence.** In October 1948 the Directorate of Intelligence asked the DCI to send any available evidence "which might serve to indicate that these objects have a domestic origin" <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={22} q="might serve to indicate that these objects have a domestic origin." />. The Air Force was asking whether the objects might be American.

**December 1947: the earlier analysis.** An earlier Directorate of Intelligence analysis in the same file reaches a parallel position. It says "The final answer still has not been obtained" from the foreign-origin line of inquiry <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={244} q="The final answer still has not been obtained" />, and concludes: "On the basis of presently available information, if these" discs "actually exist they are foreign in origin" <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={244} q="discs actually exist they are foreign in origin, so investigation of" />. The same section notes that sightings were not reported at or near the principal strategic target areas of the United States <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={244} q="at or near the principal strategic target areas of the United" />. In its list of typical reports, the analysis dates Kenneth Arnold's sighting to 29 July 1947 <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={241} q="On 29 July, 1947, Kenneth Arnold" />; the entry on [Kenneth Arnold](/finds/kenneth-arnold-june-24-1947) dates it to 24 June. The same page lists "William Rhoads of Phoenix" as a photographer <Cite card="da533c72f476d84f" page={241} q="William Rhoads of Phoenix" />, whom the entry on [the Rhodes photographs](/finds/rhodes-photographs-phoenix-1947) treats under a different spelling.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming Project Sign found extraterrestrial craft, and neither did it. The file's own text is clear that no physical evidence existed and that the nature of the objects could not be established without it. We are also not claiming the foreign-origin reading was right. We read the file as a record of how one office reasoned in 1947 and 1948 with the information it had. The conclusions quoted are those of Air Force offices, not ours. A "Twining opinion" memo of 1947 also appears inside D100, but it is already covered by an earlier release, and we do not lead with it. The 1946 "ghost rocket" intelligence review released alongside D100 is not part of this entry. Every quotation above is verbatim from the released OCR, pinned to its card and page, with hyphenation at line breaks kept as printed. The selection and framing are ours.
