## What the document is

**DOW-UAP-D105** (`fec5b139eb7b0a4f`), *U.S. Air Force Flying Discs File, 1952*, is 253 pages of Headquarters USAF correspondence from the Directorate of Intelligence's Topical Division. Most of it is routed through Maj. Fournet's desk (AFOIN-2A2). Much of the file is form replies to citizens. A few pages cover physical evidence: things someone could put under a microscope. This entry reads those pages.

The best-known item is the cap from the DesVergers case. The file records it as the alleged sighting "in the vicinity of West Palm Beach, Florida on the night of 19 August 1952" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={238} q="flying object in the vicinity of West Palm Beach, Florida on the night of" />. The witness is named throughout the file. We give his name as the documents do, and nothing more about him.

## The FBI Laboratory report

The report is addressed to the Air Force's Directorate of Special Investigations. It is dated 3 September 1952 and carries the signature block "s/J. Edgar Hoover" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="s/J. Edgar Hoover" />. The specimen is listed as "Q1", the "Cap which Mr. Desvergers was wearing at the time of the alleged incident" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="Cap which Mr. Desvergers was wearing at the time of the alleged" />. Its findings come in four steps.

**No residue.** The Air Force had asked what burned the holes. The lab's answer:

> "the areas surrounding the three burned holes in the cap were examined for any residues which might indicate the nature of the incendiary material causing the burns, but no such residues were found." <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="but no such residues were found." />

The lab also ran a control. On the sweatband it found that residue from "a glowing ember used to burn a hole in the cloth was easily dislodged and removed by handling" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="indicate that the residue from a glowing ember used to burn a hole in the cloth" />. In other words, the absence of residue does not rule out an ordinary burn.

**The wrong pattern for a blast from above.** The singeing "is not at all uniform as might be expected" from a single shot of fire <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="that the singeing on these areas is not at all uniform as might be expected" />, and the front edge of the bill is scorched worst:

> "The fact that the front edge of the cap bill is more severely scorched or singed than other portions of the cap suggests that the singeing could not have taken place when Mr. D. S. Desvergers was fired upon from a position almost vertically above him." <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={176} q="could not have taken place when Mr. D. S. Desvergers was fired upon from a" />

**Not on his head.** A fold in the cloth near the insignia flattens out when the cap is worn. There was no singeing under the fold, so:

> "the absence of singeing under the fold suggests the possibility that the cap was not being worn on the head when the singeing took place." <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={177} q="the absence of singeing under the fold suggests the" />

**One small burn it cannot pin on anyone.** A burned spot about 1/32 inch across on the bill "is believed to be too small to have been caused singularly and intentionally by Mr. Desvergers" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={177} q="small to have been caused singularly and intentionally by Mr. Desvergers." />. The lab suggests instead "a small glowing ember of some kind, very hot but containing a small amount of heat" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={177} q="glowing ember of some kind, very hot but containing a small amount of heat," />.

The lab does not use the word hoax. It says what the burns are not consistent with.

## What the Air Force did with it

On 19 September 1952 Headquarters forwarded the report and the cap to the Air Technical Intelligence Center. The cover memo decides the cap's fate: "Since it was necessary to mark and cut the cap for analysis", it is "inadvisable to return it to Mr. DesVergers" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={175} q="Since it was necessary to mark and cut the cap for analysis, it is con-" />. Instead, "It is believed preferrable to offer a replacement" ("preferrable" [sic]) if the Air Force had agreed to return it <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={175} q="It is believed preferrable" />.

A Directorate memo of 5 September, asking the Directorate of Special Investigations to obtain further records, states the working view. ATIC's field investigation had not been conclusive, "However, there are indications of a possible hoax", citing "the results of the FBI lab analysis on Des Vergers' cap" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={238} q="However, there are indications of a possible hoax, viz. the results" />. That memo goes on into the witness's personal history. We don't reproduce that material here.

To the public the Air Force said less. A November 1952 reply to a citizen who had written to the President says "a thorough investigation of the incident has been conducted", that the sensational aspects of the press stories "were exaggerated" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={47} q="inal stories carried by the national press agencies were exaggerated." />, and that "No facts have been withheld from the public" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={47} q="No facts have been withheld from the" />. It does not mention the lab report.

## Arlington and Flatwoods: the soil

The same autumn, the Topical Division was trying to test ground traces. The file holds a list of exhibits from a "perfect circle" in a residential yard in Arlington, Virginia, on 1–2 September 1952: "Soil and grass sample from circle", samples from the center, leaves, and three nearby fertilizers for comparison <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={235} q="Soil and grass sample from circle (at A on Incl 1)" />.

Then came Flatwoods, West Virginia, 12 September 1952. A memo written 30 September explains that a press report attributed to a National Guard officer the remark that "skid marks" on a hillside "were formed by a greasy deposit which felt like graphite". The Air Force saw a "possible relation" to Arlington and asked him for samples <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={141} q="the hill were formed by a greasy deposit which felt like graphite." />. The same memo reports the result:

> "Samples arrived 29 Sept 52 and show no evidence of a graphite-like deposit." <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={141} q="Samples arrived 29 Sept 52 and show no evidence of a graphite-like deposit." />

It also notes, dryly, that a drawing of the Flatwoods "monster" shown on television is "vaguely reminiscent of a character which appeared in a comic strip a few years ago" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={141} q="a character which appeared in a comic strip a few years ago." />.

Two days after that memo was written, the Division asked the Inspector General's investigators to hurry the lab analysis of earlier soil samples:

> "Recent developments have prompted the attachment of increased importance to these samples. Specifically, a deposit seeming to have the same general characteristics and also related to a flying object incident has been reported from Flat Woods, West Virginia." <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={127} q="Specifically, a deposit seeming to have the same general characteristics and also" />

It asks "that utmost priority be given to the lab analysis of subject samples" <Cite card="fec5b139eb7b0a4f" page={127} q="of these two incidents, it is requested that utmost priority be given to the lab" />. The memo does not name the earlier samples. The file, as far as we read it, does not include the result.

## What we're not claiming

We are not claiming DesVergers staged the incident. The FBI Laboratory did not say so. It said the burns did not fit the account as given, and that the cap was probably not being worn when it was singed. An Air Force memo read that as an indication of a possible hoax. That is the Air Force's inference, recorded in its own file, not a finding we endorse or reject. We are not claiming anything about what was, or wasn't, in the Arlington or Flatwoods soil. The only result in these pages is the Flatwoods observation that the samples showed no graphite-like deposit. The priority request came after it, and the file does not explain why. We have left out the witness's personal records, which the file includes, along with the names and addresses of private correspondents and property owners.
